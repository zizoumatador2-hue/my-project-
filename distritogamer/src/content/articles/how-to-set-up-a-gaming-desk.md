Your desk setup quietly shapes how well and how long you can play. A monitor that is too low strains your neck, a keyboard too far away makes your shoulders creep up, and a cramped mouse area forces you to lift and reposition at the wrong moments. None of these needs expensive equipment to fix.

This guide shows you how to arrange the setup you already have, then explains where small purchases can make a difference. It supports advice in our guides on [monitor settings](/game-settings/best-gaming-monitor-settings/), [mouse choice](/gaming-gear/best-gaming-mouse-for-fps/) and [headsets](/gaming-gear/best-gaming-headset-guide/), and our [complete gaming tips guide](/#pillar).

> **Note:** This is general information about comfort, not medical advice. If you have persistent pain, numbness, tingling or eye discomfort, stop and speak to a qualified professional. See [ergonomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_factors_and_ergonomics) for background.

## Start with your body, not the equipment

Set up from the chair outward:

1. **Feet:** flat on the floor or a footrest.
2. **Knees and thighs:** thighs roughly level with the floor, knees around 90 degrees.
3. **Back:** supported, especially the lower back, with a relaxed upright posture.
4. **Arms:** elbows near 90 degrees and close to your body, shoulders relaxed, forearms roughly level.
5. **Wrists:** straight, not bent up or down.

If your desk is too high for this posture, raise your chair and use a footrest. If it is too low, consider raising the desk.

## Monitor height and distance

- **Height:** the top edge of the screen should be around eye level, so you look slightly downward at the center. A monitor too low causes you to hunch.
- **Distance:** about an arm's length is a good start. Increase it for larger screens.
- **Tilt and angle:** tilt slightly back, so there is no glare and your neck stays neutral.
- **Centering:** center the screen in front of you, not off to one side.

A VESA monitor arm gives precise control over height and distance and frees desk space. Check that the arm supports your monitor's weight and mount pattern.

## Keyboard and mouse

- **Keyboard:** close enough that you do not reach, with wrists straight. A keyboard without a big incline can help keep wrists neutral.
- **Mouse:** at the same height as the keyboard, close to your body.
- **Mouse space:** leave enough room to swipe at your sensitivity without hitting the keyboard or the desk edge. Players who use low sensitivity need a larger pad. See our [sensitivity guide](/game-settings/best-sensitivity-settings-for-gaming/).
- **Pad:** a large, consistent surface. Edges that stand on the desk edge can catch your wrist, so choose a pad with flat stitched edges.

If your desk is shallow, consider whether a smaller keyboard leaves more mouse room.

## Chair choice

You do not need a "gaming" label. Look for:

- Adjustable seat height
- Lumbar support that fits your lower back
- Armrests that adjust or move out of the way
- A seat depth that does not press on the back of your knees

Judge any chair by sitting in it for a long time, not just a short test.

## Lighting and glare

- **Avoid reflections:** place the monitor so that windows and lamps do not reflect in the screen.
- **Avoid a dark room with a bright screen:** a soft light behind or beside the monitor reduces eye strain.
- **Match screen brightness to the room.** See our [monitor settings guide](/game-settings/best-gaming-monitor-settings/).
- **Warm lighting in the evening** is more comfortable for many people.

## Cables and tidiness

A tidy desk is easier to use. Route cables behind the desk or along a channel, label chargers, and leave slack for movement. A wired mouse should have an easy path that does not drag. Make sure your PC has airflow, with space around vents so it does not overheat. See [how to increase FPS on PC](/gaming-performance/how-to-increase-fps-on-pc/) for thermal tips.

## Audio setup

Put speakers at ear level or use a headset that you find comfortable. If you wear a headset for hours, check pressure on your head and ears. See the [headset guide](/gaming-gear/best-gaming-headset-guide/) for what to look for. Keep a clear spot for a microphone if you use one.

## Break habits

Even a perfect setup is not meant to be used without rest.

- **Eyes:** a common suggestion, the 20-20-20 rule, is to look at something about 20 feet away for around 20 seconds every 20 minutes. See [computer vision syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision_syndrome) for background.
- **Body:** stand, stretch and walk between matches.
- **Hands and wrists:** gentle stretches help, and sharp or persistent pain is a signal to stop. See [repetitive strain injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repetitive_strain_injury).
- **Hydration and food:** keep water nearby.

Pair this with a simple pre-game ritual like our [warm-up routine](/gaming-tips/how-to-build-a-gaming-warm-up-routine/) to keep your hands loose.

## Small upgrades that can matter

| Problem | Possible fix |
|---|---|
| Monitor too low | Monitor arm or a stable stand |
| Desk too cramped | Larger desk or a monitor arm to free space |
| Mouse runs out of room | Larger pad or lower sensitivity |
| Neck or back discomfort | Chair adjustments, lumbar support, footrest |
| Glare | Reposition screen, add soft lighting |
| Cable clutter | Cable channels and ties |

Spend on the problem you actually have, not on a "battlestation" look.

## Common mistakes

- **Looking down at a low screen.** It strains your neck.
- **Reaching for the keyboard or mouse.** It tires your shoulders.
- **A tiny mouse area.** It forces repositioning.
- **Ignoring breaks.** Even good posture is tiring when static.
- **Buying expensive gear before adjusting what you own.**

## A step-by-step setup check you can do in 15 minutes

If you want a quick way to improve your setup without spending anything, run this check.

1. **Sit down as you normally play.** Do not adjust anything yet.
2. **Check your feet.** Are they flat on the floor? If not, raise the chair or find a footrest.
3. **Check your elbows.** Are they near 90 degrees and close to your body? If your shoulders are lifted, lower the chair's armrests or raise the chair relative to the desk.
4. **Check your wrists.** Are they straight, or bent up or down? Adjust the keyboard height or tilt.
5. **Check your screen.** Is the top of the screen near eye level, and are you looking straight at it? Raise it with a stand or monitor arm if you are looking down.
6. **Check your mouse area.** Can you do a full-speed swipe at your sensitivity without hitting anything? If not, clear space or lower your sensitivity.
7. **Check glare.** Look at the screen with it off. Do you see reflections of lamps or windows? Move the screen or the light.
8. **Check your breaks.** Set a timer or use your game's natural pauses to stand up and look away from the screen.

Do this every few months, because small changes such as a new chair or monitor can alter the whole arrangement.

## Setting up for different play styles

Different games and inputs favor different arrangements.

- **Low-sensitivity mouse players** need a large pad and plenty of desk depth, so they can sweep their arm. A smaller keyboard can free space.
- **High-sensitivity or wrist-aim players** need less room but still benefit from a pad with a consistent surface.
- **Controller players on a desk** should keep the controller at a comfortable height, supporting their elbows. Many players find it easier to sit back with the controller in their lap.
- **Couch players** can use a lap tray or a low table so that they are not holding their arms up.
- **Players with several monitors** should keep the main monitor straight ahead and place the others slightly angled, so that they do not twist their neck.

## Sound, noise and shared spaces

If you share a room, your setup affects other people as well.

- **A closed-back headset** keeps game sound in and room noise out.
- **A quieter keyboard or desk mat** reduces noise, which matters if you play at night.
- **Fan noise:** check the PC's fan curves and airflow, and keep the PC off the floor if there is dust.
- **Lighting:** a soft lamp avoids waking people while helping your eyes.

A pleasant, quiet setup is easier to play in for long periods, and it makes the people you live with happier too.

## Upgrades by priority

If you do want to spend money, the following order usually gives the best return:

1. **Chair adjustments or a better chair,** if your back or legs hurt.
2. **Monitor height,** through a stand or arm.
3. **A larger mouse pad,** if your mouse runs out of room.
4. **Lighting,** if you suffer glare or eye strain.
5. **Cable management,** for a cleaner, safer space.

Aesthetic upgrades such as lights and decorations are fine if you enjoy them, but they do not change how you play.

## Adjust as you go

Your ideal setup will change as your games, sensitivity and body change. If you notice a new ache, a reach or a squint, treat it as a signal to adjust something rather than something to put up with. A five-minute tweak to chair height, monitor tilt or mouse position is almost always cheaper and more effective than a new piece of equipment.

## Key takeaways

- Start with your body: feet supported, elbows near 90 degrees, shoulders relaxed and wrists straight.
- Put the top of the monitor near eye level at about an arm's length, and avoid glare.
- Leave generous room for mouse movement, especially at low sensitivity.
- Take regular breaks to stand, stretch and rest your eyes.
- Fix the problem you actually have before buying anything for the look of the setup.

A comfortable setup is one you stop noticing. When it is right, you can play longer, practice better and finish sessions feeling fine.
