Not every gaming night needs a lobby, a voice chat and a ranked match. **Single player games** let you play at your own pace, pause whenever you want and enjoy a story or a challenge built only for you. PC is one of the best places to play them: the library is huge, older classics are cheap, and you can tune settings to your hardware.

This guide lists twelve of the best single player games on PC, grouped by style. For each one you get what it asks of you, who it suits and one honest drawback. If your PC struggles with newer releases, read our guide on [how to increase FPS on PC](/gaming-performance/how-to-increase-fps-on-pc/) before you buy.

## How we chose these games

We looked for games that are widely praised, still easy to buy on PC and good at something specific. We avoided games that depend on online multiplayer to be fun. We also tried to cover different budgets of time: some picks take a hundred hours, others take a weekend.

This is not a ranking of "the best game ever". Taste matters. The goal is to help you find the right game for the time and mood you have.

## Open-world adventures

### Elden Ring

Elden Ring is a large fantasy world with tough combat and very little hand-holding. You explore freely, find secrets on your own and fight bosses that demand patience. It suits players who enjoy learning from failure.

**Drawback:** the difficulty can feel harsh at first. Our [Elden Ring guide for beginners](/game-guides/beginners-guide-to-elden-ring/) covers the systems that help most in the opening hours.

### The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt

The Witcher 3 is remembered for its side quests, which often feel as carefully written as the main story. Combat is simpler than in Elden Ring, and lower difficulties are forgiving. It suits players who want a long story with meaningful choices.

**Drawback:** the opening area is slow, and the inventory can feel cluttered.

## Story-driven RPGs

### Baldur's Gate 3

Baldur's Gate 3 is a turn-based role-playing game based on Dungeons & Dragons rules. You can solve most problems in several ways: talking, sneaking, fighting or using the environment. It suits players who enjoy planning and reacting to consequences.

**Drawback:** it is long and asks for a fairly capable PC, especially in its later acts.

### Disco Elysium

Disco Elysium has almost no traditional combat. You play a detective, and the game is built on dialogue, skills and choices. It suits readers and players who enjoy strong writing.

**Drawback:** there is a lot of text, so it is not for players who want action.

## Strategy games

### Civilization VI

Civilization VI is a turn-based strategy game where you guide a civilisation through history. Each turn is short, but a full game takes many hours, which is why "one more turn" is such a common joke. It suits players who like long-term planning.

**Drawback:** later turns can slow down on large maps.

### XCOM 2

XCOM 2 is tactical, turn-based combat with a strategic layer between missions. Every soldier matters, and losing one hurts. It suits players who like tense decisions. For general ideas that carry across strategy games, see [gaming strategies that work in any game](/advanced-strategies/gaming-strategies-that-work-in-any-game/).

**Drawback:** hit chances can feel unfair, and the timer on some missions frustrates new players.

## Shooters with strong campaigns

### Doom Eternal

Doom Eternal is fast, aggressive first-person combat. The game rewards constant movement and smart weapon switching. It suits players who want pure action.

**Drawback:** the pace is relentless, which can be tiring in long sessions. A stable frame rate helps a lot here, so check our [best FPS settings guide](/game-settings/best-fps-settings-for-better-performance/).

### Half-Life 2

Half-Life 2 is an older shooter that still holds up thanks to its pacing, physics puzzles and memorable set pieces. It runs on almost any modern PC. It suits players who want a classic story shooter.

**Drawback:** its graphics show their age next to modern games.

## Action and platformers

### Hades

Hades is a roguelike: you fight through the underworld, die, return stronger and try again. Runs are short, so it fits busy schedules. It suits players who enjoy fast combat and gradual progress.

**Drawback:** the loop of repeated runs is not for everyone.

### Hollow Knight

Hollow Knight is a hand-drawn action platformer with a large connected map. Exploration and precise combat are at its core. It suits players who like discovering a world piece by piece.

**Drawback:** getting lost is part of the design, which can frustrate some players.

## Shorter games for a weekend

### Portal 2

Portal 2 is a puzzle game where you solve rooms with a portal gun. It is funny, clever and finishes in a reasonable time. It is one of the easiest recommendations for any player.

**Drawback:** once you know the solutions, there is less reason to replay it.

### Celeste

Celeste is a challenging platformer about climbing a mountain. Its controls are precise, and it includes an assist mode that lets you lower the difficulty. It suits players who enjoy mastering hard sections.

**Drawback:** some later levels are very demanding without assist options.

## How to pick the right single player game

Ask three simple questions before you buy:

1. **How much time do you have?** If you play in short bursts, choose Hades, Portal 2 or Celeste. If you have long evenings, an open-world game or a large RPG will keep you busy for weeks.
2. **What kind of challenge do you enjoy?** Elden Ring, Hollow Knight and Celeste test reflexes and patience. Disco Elysium, Civilization VI and Baldur's Gate 3 test decisions and planning.
3. **Can your PC run it well?** Check the minimum and recommended requirements on the store page. A game that stutters is less fun, however good it is. If you see drops, our guide to [fixing game lag and stutter](/troubleshooting/how-to-fix-game-lag/) can help.

### Controller or mouse and keyboard?

Many single player games play well with either. Action games such as Elden Ring, Hollow Knight and Celeste often feel better on a controller, while strategy games and shooters usually suit mouse and keyboard. ## Getting the best performance in single player games

Single player games do not need the very low input lag that competitive shooters chase, but a smooth and stable frame rate still makes them far more enjoyable. A few simple steps help on most PCs:

- **Update your graphics driver** before you start a new game. New releases often get driver fixes in their first weeks.
- **Choose a preset, then adjust.** Start with the "High" or "Medium" preset and lower the most expensive options first, usually shadows, volumetric effects and ray tracing.
- **Use upscaling if it is offered.** Options such as DLSS, FSR or XeSS can raise frame rates a lot with a small loss in sharpness.
- **Cap your frame rate** a little below your monitor's refresh rate if you see uneven frame pacing. Steady frames feel smoother than a number that jumps up and down.
- **Turn on Windows Game Mode** and close heavy background apps. Our guide to [optimizing Windows for gaming](/gaming-performance/how-to-optimize-windows-for-gaming/) explains which tweaks are safe.

If a game crashes or stutters even after these steps, follow our [crash and stutter troubleshooting guide](/troubleshooting/fix-game-crashes-and-stuttering/).

## Where to buy single player games on PC

Most of these games are sold on several PC stores, and prices change often. Big seasonal sales regularly discount older single player games, so it is worth adding the games you want to a wishlist and waiting if you are not in a hurry. Buy from official stores to avoid revoked keys and account problems.

## Tips to enjoy single player games more

- **Lower the difficulty without guilt.** Difficulty settings exist so more people can finish the game. Play the way that makes it fun for you.
- **Avoid spoilers.** Story games are best when you discover them yourself. Mute keywords on social media while you play.
- **Take breaks in long games.** Coming back after a day often makes a hard boss or puzzle easier.
- **Play one game at a time.** Starting five games at once makes it harder to finish any of them.
- **Tune your settings once.** Spend ten minutes on graphics and controls at the start, then forget about them. Our [gaming tips for beginners](/beginner-guides/gaming-tips-for-beginners/) cover the basics.

## Final thoughts

The best single player game on PC is the one that matches your time, your taste and your hardware. If you want a safe first pick, start with Portal 2. If you want a long adventure, try The Witcher 3 or Elden Ring. If you like thinking more than reacting, choose Baldur's Gate 3, Disco Elysium or Civilization VI. Whatever you choose, play at your own pace. That is the whole point of playing alone.
