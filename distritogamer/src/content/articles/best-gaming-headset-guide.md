A good headset does two jobs in a team game. It tells you where footsteps, gunfire and abilities are coming from, and it lets your team hear you clearly. A bad one is uncomfortable after an hour, muddy or boomy, and has a microphone that makes you sound like you are in a bin.

This guide explains what to look for and what to ignore when choosing a gaming headset. We do not rank models, because we have not tested them side by side for this guide. Instead, we give you the criteria so that you can compare any headset fairly, and our [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/) explains how we handle links. ## Comfort comes first

You will wear a headset for hours, so comfort matters more than any specification.

- **Weight and clamping force:** a heavy or tight headset becomes tiring. Look for a balanced weight and a headband that distributes pressure.
- **Earpad material:** fabric breathes better, while leather-style pads isolate sound but get warm. Pads that are too small press on the ears.
- **Glasses:** if you wear glasses, softer pads and a lower clamping force help.
- **Head size:** the headband should adjust to your head without extremes.

Try a headset if you can, and always check a retailer's return policy.

## Sound: clarity beats bass

Many gaming headsets are tuned with heavy bass, which makes explosions exciting but can mask footsteps and voices. Look for independent measurements or reviews that comment on balance and clarity.

- **Clear mids and highs** help you hear footsteps and cues.
- **Controlled bass** gives impact without drowning other sounds.
- **A natural stereo image** helps you locate sounds left and right.

### Open-back versus closed-back

| Type | Sound character | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Closed-back | Isolates outside noise, keeps sound in | Can feel less spacious and warmer |
| Open-back | Spacious, natural soundstage | Leaks sound and lets room noise in; not ideal for shared spaces or voice chat |

If you play in a quiet room alone, open-back can sound excellent. In a shared room or if your microphone is near the headset, closed-back is a safer choice. Learn the basics in [headphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headphones).

## Surround sound: useful or hype?

Games already generate **positional audio** in stereo. Virtual 7.1 surround modes process that sound again to simulate more speakers. Some players find it helps with vertical and rear positioning, while others find it blurs sounds or changes the tone. A practical approach:

1. Start with plain stereo and your game's default audio setting.
2. Try a virtual surround option for a few games.
3. Keep whichever makes it easier to locate footsteps in your favorite game.

## Microphone

Your teammates hear you through the headset's microphone, so quality matters.

- **Clarity:** listen to independent recordings if you can find them, because specs do not tell you much.
- **Boom or detachable microphone:** a boom microphone near your mouth usually sounds better than one built into the earcup.
- **Noise handling:** some headsets offer noise suppression. Software noise suppression is also available in many voice chat apps.
- **Mute indicator:** an easy mute switch or light avoids embarrassing moments.

If you already own good headphones, adding a separate microphone can work well.

## Wired or wireless

- **Wired:** simple, no battery, no pairing. Check that it has the right connector for your device, such as a 3.5 mm plug or USB.
- **Wireless with a dongle:** removes cable clutter and usually has low delay. Check battery life honestly against your play sessions.
- **Bluetooth:** convenient but often adds latency and may disable the microphone or reduce quality. Not ideal for fast competitive play.

For consoles, check compatibility with your specific system, because some wireless headsets only work with certain platforms. On PC, almost any headset works over USB or 3.5 mm.

## Impedance, drivers and amps

You will see numbers like 32 ohms or 50 mm drivers. For typical gaming headsets, these do not need your attention: headsets designed for consumers work with PCs, consoles and phones without extra equipment. Only very high-impedance audiophile headphones need an amplifier. A bigger driver size does not guarantee better sound.

## Protect your hearing

Loud audio over long periods can cause permanent hearing damage; see [noise-induced hearing loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise-induced_hearing_loss) for background. Sensible habits:

- Keep volume at a level where you can still hear a person speaking next to you.
- Take regular breaks, and give your ears time to rest.
- If you notice ringing or muffled hearing, stop and consider seeing a professional.

This is general advice, not medical advice.

## Setting up your audio

- **In-game audio mix:** many games let you adjust the balance between effects, music and voice. Lower music to hear footsteps better.
- **Dynamic range or night mode options:** these can reduce loud peaks, useful for quieter play.
- **Chat levels:** set voice chat loud enough to understand but not so loud it hides game sound.
- **Equalizer:** use sparingly. A small change can help, and extreme settings often hurt.

## What you can ignore

- **Frequency response numbers** on the box, such as 20 Hz to 20 kHz
- **Brand claims about "pro" tuning** without independent measurements
- **RGB lighting**, unless you like it
- **Large driver size** as a sign of quality

## A simple buying process

1. **Decide your platform** and connection.
2. **Set a budget** and whether you want wireless.
3. **Shortlist** comfortable headsets with clear independent reviews of sound and microphone.
4. **Check return policy and warranty.**
5. **Test at home** with a game you know well, listening for footsteps and checking your microphone with a friend.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying for bass.** It may be fun but can hurt clarity.
- **Ignoring comfort.** A headset you take off after an hour is a poor purchase.
- **Assuming surround sound is better.** It is a feature, not a guarantee.
- **Playing too loud.** It risks long-term damage.
- **Forgetting platform compatibility** on consoles.

## Testing a headset at home

Specifications tell you little, so test what matters. Within the return period, try the following.

1. **Wear it for an hour or two** and notice pressure points, heat and weight.
2. **Listen in a game you know well,** and see whether you can place footsteps and abilities easily.
3. **Check for fatigue.** Sharp, bright sound can tire your ears, and heavy bass can be tiring too.
4. **Record your microphone** and listen to the recording. Ask a friend how you sound in voice chat.
5. **Test the connection** for dropouts, delay and battery life if wireless.
6. **Compare with your current headset** or speakers, if you have them, with the same game and volume.

If a headset passes these tests, it is a good fit for you, whatever the reviews say.

## Console and platform notes

Compatibility can be confusing, especially on consoles.

- **Wired headsets with a 3.5 mm plug** often work on controllers and many devices, but check that the plug type (the number of rings) matches.
- **USB headsets** work on many systems but not all.
- **Wireless headsets** often use a dongle designed for specific platforms, so check which consoles it supports.
- **Bluetooth** is generally not supported for game audio on many consoles.
- **Chat and game balance** controls may vary by platform.

Before you buy, check the product page and your console's support information for compatibility.

## Using headphones plus a separate microphone

Some players prefer a pair of quality headphones and a separate microphone. The advantages are flexibility and often better sound, and the downside is more cables and setup. If you choose this route:

- Pick a microphone with a pickup pattern suited to your room, so that it picks up your voice rather than keyboard noise.
- Place it close to your mouth, off to the side so that it does not catch breath sounds.
- Use a pop filter or a foam cover if plosive sounds are a problem.

Whether you use a headset or separate equipment, the aim is the same: to hear clearly and to be heard clearly.

## Headset care and lifespan

A little care makes a headset last longer and stay comfortable.

- **Clean the earpads** with a slightly damp cloth, and replace them when they flatten or crack. Many models have replacement pads.
- **Store the headset** on a stand or hook instead of leaving it on the floor.
- **Handle the cable and connector gently** if it is wired, and avoid sharp bends.
- **Charge wireless headsets** before they are completely empty when you can, and follow the manufacturer's advice for battery care.
- **Check for firmware updates** if your headset has companion software.

## Key takeaways

- Comfort over several hours matters more than any feature on the box.
- Look for clear, balanced sound rather than heavy bass, so footsteps and voices stay audible.
- Check microphone quality with a real recording, and prefer a boom or detachable microphone.
- Wired is simple and reliable, wireless adds freedom, and Bluetooth is usually a poor fit for fast competitive play.
- Keep volume moderate and take breaks to protect your hearing.

Test any headset at home with a game you know well, and use the return period if it does not suit you.
