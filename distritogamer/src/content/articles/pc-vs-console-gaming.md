PC or console is one of the oldest debates in gaming, and most of it is noise. The better question is not which is best, but which is best **for you**: the games you want to play, the money you have to spend, who you play with and whether you enjoy tweaking hardware or would rather press a button and play.

This comparison lays out the trade-offs honestly. We do not rank products or claim hands-on testing for specific hardware, and prices change often, so we avoid quoting numbers that would be out of date. For wider help, see our [complete gaming tips guide](/#pillar) and our [gaming tips for beginners](/beginner-guides/gaming-tips-for-beginners/).

## The short version

| Factor | Console | PC |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Lower and predictable | Higher for equivalent performance, but flexible |
| Setup | Plug in and play | More setup and decisions |
| Game library | Platform-specific exclusives and a curated store | Very large library across multiple stores, plus older and indie games |
| Performance | Fixed hardware, optimized targets | Scales with your hardware and settings |
| Controls | Controller is standard | Mouse and keyboard, controller and more |
| Upgrades | Replace the console each generation | Upgrade individual parts |
| Online play | Often subscription-based | Free on many platforms, with some exceptions |
| Mods and customization | Limited | Extensive in many games |

## Cost: look at the whole picture

**Console:** the entry price is lower, and every console runs every game made for it. You may need to pay for online multiplayer on some platforms and extra storage later.

**PC:** the hardware costs more, especially if you want console-level or better performance. But you may also save on game prices through sales and multiple stores, and you will likely use the same PC for other things. You can upgrade one part at a time, which spreads the cost.

Be careful with simple claims. A cheap PC may run older and lighter games well, and a console gives better performance per dollar for many modern games. Look at your actual list of games and the platform requirements before you decide.

## Games: what do you actually want to play?

- **Platform exclusives:** some games are available only on a particular console, and some only on PC. Check that your must-play games are on the platform you pick.
- **Library size:** PC has a very large library, including older games, indie titles and strategy games that benefit from a mouse.
- **Genres:** shooters, strategy and simulation games often feel better with a mouse and keyboard, while platformers, sports and action games are very comfortable with a controller.
- **Free-to-play:** many popular free games are on both, such as [Valorant](/game-guides/beginners-guide-to-valorant/) on PC and others on all platforms.

Before buying anything, make a list of the ten games you most want to play and check where each is available.

## Performance and settings

Console games are built for fixed hardware, so you get a predictable experience, often with a choice between a performance mode and a quality mode. On PC, you have control: you can adjust settings, resolution and frame rate to match your hardware and taste. That control is powerful but takes some learning; see our guides to [FPS settings](/game-settings/best-fps-settings-for-better-performance/) and [increasing FPS](/gaming-performance/how-to-increase-fps-on-pc/). A PC can reach higher frame rates and resolutions than a console, if the hardware can handle it, and the display must support it, as covered in our [monitor settings guide](/game-settings/best-gaming-monitor-settings/).

## Controls and competitive play

Consoles are built around controllers. PCs support controllers as well, plus mouse and keyboard, which many players find more precise for aiming. Competitive games often match players by input type, or offer aim assist for controller players to balance things. If you are interested in this difference, read our comparison of [controller versus mouse and keyboard](/game-comparisons/controller-vs-mouse-and-keyboard/) and our guide to [controller settings](/game-settings/best-controller-settings-for-competitive-gaming/).

## Ease of use and maintenance

- **Console:** install games, update, play. Troubleshooting is simpler because every console is the same.
- **PC:** you will deal with drivers, updates, settings and sometimes troubleshooting. Many people enjoy this, and others find it frustrating. See [how to optimize Windows for gaming](/gaming-performance/how-to-optimize-windows-for-gaming/) and [fix crashes and stuttering](/troubleshooting/fix-game-crashes-and-stuttering/) for what that looks like.

## Online play and friends

Consoles tend to have integrated friends lists and party chat, and some require a paid subscription for online multiplayer. PC platforms generally have free online play, and you may use separate apps for chat. Some games support **cross-platform play**, letting you play with friends on other platforms; see [cross-platform play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-platform_play). Always check the specific game.

Whichever you choose, a stable connection matters. See [how to fix game lag](/troubleshooting/how-to-fix-game-lag/).

## Mods, customization and extras

PC gaming offers extensive modding, custom controllers and the ability to use the machine for other tasks, such as creating content, working or streaming. Some console games support mods, but the options are narrower. If modding matters to you, PC is the stronger platform.

## Living room versus desk

Consoles are designed for a TV and a couch, which many people prefer. PCs are often used at a desk with a monitor and a mouse and keyboard, but many players connect them to a TV. Think about where you want to play and with whom.

## Who should choose what?

**A console may suit you if you:**

- Want a simple, predictable setup
- Mostly play with a controller
- Play exclusives available on that console
- Prefer a lower upfront cost
- Play in a living room

**A PC may suit you if you:**

- Enjoy customizing settings and hardware
- Play genres that favor a mouse and keyboard
- Want the widest game library, including older titles
- Want mods
- Want a machine that also handles other tasks

**You may want both** if you play different games on each, or share with others at home.

## A quick decision process

1. **List your must-play games** and check platform availability.
2. **Decide on your controls:** controller or mouse and keyboard.
3. **Set a total budget,** including a display, storage and any subscriptions.
4. **Think about where you will play** and with whom.
5. **Decide how much tinkering you enjoy.**

If the answers point both ways, start with the platform that has your most-wanted games and revisit later.

## Common myths

- **"PC is always better."** It depends on your needs and budget.
- **"Consoles are for casual players."** Many serious and competitive players use them.
- **"You need the most expensive parts."** Many popular games run well on modest hardware.
- **"Console games all look worse."** Many look excellent, and the difference depends on the settings and display.

## Costs beyond the console or the PC

The headline price is only part of the cost. Make a list that includes everything you will need.

| Item | Console | PC |
|---|---|---|
| Main hardware | One purchase | One purchase, or parts bought over time |
| Display | Often an existing TV | Monitor, or a TV |
| Controller | Included, with extras optional | Controller optional, mouse and keyboard needed |
| Online play | Subscription on some platforms | Usually free |
| Games | Platform stores and physical copies | Several stores, frequent sales |
| Storage | Expansion may be needed | Storage is upgradeable |
| Upgrades | A new console each generation | Parts over time |

Do not assume one is always cheaper. A budget PC can be cheaper than a console in some cases, and a console can be cheaper than a PC that gives equal performance in others. The honest approach is to price your whole setup and the games you want.

## Questions to ask before you buy

1. Which games do I most want to play, and where are they available?
2. Do I prefer a controller or a mouse and keyboard?
3. Where will I play, and do I need a screen of my own?
4. Do my friends play on a particular platform?
5. How much do I enjoy tinkering, and how much do I want things to just work?
6. How long do I want this setup to last?

Write down your answers. Most people find that two or three of them settle the question.

## Performance modes and what they mean

On consoles, many games offer a choice of modes, often described as a performance mode with a higher frame rate and a quality mode with higher resolution or better effects. If you play competitive or fast games, a higher frame rate usually feels better. For slower or story-driven games, many players prefer the better visuals.

On PC, the equivalent is your graphics settings. See our guides on [FPS settings](/game-settings/best-fps-settings-for-better-performance/) and [input lag](/gaming-performance/how-to-reduce-input-lag-on-pc/) for how to tune things.

## Switching later is possible

Your first choice is not a life sentence. Many players start on one platform and later add another, and your account, friends and some purchases may carry across in certain cases. The skills you build, such as aiming, game sense and review habits, transfer too, as we describe in our [complete gaming tips guide](/#pillar). If you are unsure, pick the option that lets you play the games you want today, and revisit the decision when your needs change.

## Key takeaways

- Consoles are simpler, fixed and usually cheaper to start, while PCs are flexible, upgradeable and better for mods and mouse-driven games.
- Price the whole setup, including the screen, controller, storage and any subscriptions, rather than comparing headline hardware prices.
- Check that your must-play games exist on the platform before you buy.
- Think about where you will play and whether you enjoy tuning settings or want everything to just work.
- You can add a second platform later, and your skills and habits carry across.

Start from the games you want to play, then pick the platform that runs them the way you enjoy.
