*Counter-Strike 2* is Valve's free-to-play successor to *CS:GO*, released in 2023 and built on the Source 2 engine. It is a precise, patient game where small habits, such as stopping before you shoot and knowing a handful of grenade throws, decide rounds. It is also one of the best games for deliberate practice, because you can set up a private server with bots and practice whatever you need.

This guide shows you how to build that practice, in what order, and how to avoid wasting time. It works alongside our guides to [improving aim in FPS games](/gaming-tips/how-to-improve-aim-in-fps-games/), [building a warm-up routine](/gaming-tips/how-to-build-a-gaming-warm-up-routine/) and our [complete gaming tips guide](/#pillar). For a general overview, see [Counter-Strike 2 on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Strike_2).

> **Note:** Commands, map pools and modes change between updates. If something here no longer works in your version, check the in-game console and official patch notes.

## The basics of a round

In the main competitive format, two teams of five alternate between terrorists and counter-terrorists:

- **Terrorists** try to plant a bomb at a bomb site, or eliminate the other team.
- **Counter-terrorists** try to stop the plant, defuse the bomb or eliminate the other team.
- **No respawns within a round,** so every life matters.
- **Teams switch sides** at halftime, and the first to reach the winning round count takes the match.

Every round begins with a buy phase, where you spend money on weapons, armor and grenades. This economy is a core part of the strategy.

## Set up a practice environment

### Offline practice with bots

The simplest way to start is to choose the practice option with bots in the main menu. You can try weapons, learn maps and practice shooting without pressure.

### Community maps

The Steam Workshop contains community-made maps for recoil practice, aim training and grenade lineups. Search for the type of practice you need and download a popular, highly rated map.

### A private practice server

For more control, you can start a local server where cheats are allowed. This lets you change the rules to suit your practice:

1. **Enable the developer console** in the game settings and bind a key to open it.
2. **Load a map** in a local offline game.
3. **Use practice commands**, for example:
   - `sv_cheats 1` allows practice commands on your private server.
   - `sv_infinite_ammo 1` removes the need to reload during recoil practice.
   - `mp_restartgame 1` restarts the round.
   - `bot_kick` removes the bots, and `bot_stop 1` makes them stand still.

> **Important:** Practice commands work only on your own private or local server, not in matchmaking. Do not try to bypass this, because it would break the game's rules. Command names can also change in updates.

## Aim: first shot before spray

Many beginners spray as soon as they see an enemy. In Counter-Strike, accuracy depends heavily on movement and weapon behavior.

1. **Stop before you shoot.** Tap the opposite movement key to stop quickly, which is called counter-strafing, then fire.
2. **Aim for the head.** Keep your crosshair at head height as you move. See [our aim guide](/gaming-tips/how-to-improve-aim-in-fps-games/).
3. **Fire in bursts at range.** The longer you hold fire, the less accurate you become.
4. **Crouch in some situations** for accuracy, but remember it makes you easier to hit in close fights.

A simple drill: stand in front of a bot at medium range, counter-strafe and shoot a two to three bullet burst. Repeat for several minutes and keep an eye on how many shots land on the head.

## Recoil control

Each weapon has a spray pattern, a predictable path that bullets follow. Learning to pull your mouse against it keeps shots tight.

- Practice on a wall to see the pattern, then on bots.
- Learn the first few bullets, which are the most important for most fights.
- Use short sprays at long range, and full sprays when you are close.
- Practice with one rifle at a time, and add others later.

Your recoil control improves with a stable sensitivity, so keep it consistent. See [best sensitivity settings for gaming](/game-settings/best-sensitivity-settings-for-gaming/).

## Movement

Good movement makes you harder to hit and more accurate:

- **Counter-strafing** for stopping accuracy
- **Peeking** by stepping out just far enough to see an angle
- **Jiggle-peeking** to gather information without committing
- **Walking quietly** when sound matters

Practice movement in an empty practice server before you rely on it in fights.

## Utility: smokes, flashes and molotovs

Grenades are as important as aim. A few reliable throws will help you more than flashy mechanics.

- **Smoke grenades** block vision, which lets you take space or cut off an angle.
- **Flashbangs** blind opponents, letting you enter a site or win a duel.
- **Molotovs and incendiaries** deny an area or force opponents out of a position.
- **High-explosive grenades** deal damage and can clear corners.

A sensible way to learn:

1. **Pick one map** you play often.
2. **Learn two or three smokes** for your most common situations, using a practice map or server.
3. **Learn one flash** and one molotov.
4. **Repeat until consistent,** then add more.

Do not try to learn dozens of lineups. A small, reliable set is far more useful.

## Economy

Money decides what you can buy each round, so the economy is a big part of winning:

- **Full buy:** a rifle, armor and grenades.
- **Eco:** save by buying little or nothing, so the next round can be a full buy.
- **Force buy:** spend what you have when the situation demands it.
- **Team coordination:** buy together and save together, and drop weapons for teammates if the game allows it.

Learn the pistol round and the first few rounds after it, because they set the tone for each half. Our [Valorant guide](/game-guides/beginners-guide-to-valorant/) explains a similar economy idea if you want a comparison.

## Settings to check

- **Raw input:** make sure the game takes mouse input directly, with no acceleration. Disable Windows pointer acceleration too.
- **Sensitivity and crosshair:** choose a comfortable sensitivity and a clear crosshair.
- **Radar:** keep it readable and glance at it often.
- **Frame rate:** aim for a stable, high frame rate. See [best FPS settings](/game-settings/best-fps-settings-for-better-performance/) and [how to reduce input lag](/gaming-performance/how-to-reduce-input-lag-on-pc/).

## A weekly routine

| Day | Focus | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Aim and first-shot accuracy against bots | 15 to 20 min |
| Tue | Recoil control with one rifle | 15 min |
| Wed | Utility lineups on one map | 15 to 20 min |
| Thu | Aim and movement | 15 min |
| Fri | Utility and a review of one match | 20 min |
| Weekend | Play for fun and rest | Any |

After each practice session, play. Practice and play together make you improve faster than either alone. Use [our review method](/advanced-strategies/how-to-review-your-gameplay/) on your matches.

## Common mistakes

- **Spraying at long range.** Burst or tap instead.
- **Moving while shooting.** Stop first.
- **Learning too many grenade lineups.** Learn a few well.
- **Ignoring the economy.** Buy together.
- **Practicing without a goal.** Choose a measurable target.
- **Changing sensitivity constantly.** Keep it stable.

## Reading a round and communicating

Counter-Strike rewards teams that share information, so communication is part of practice.

- **Call enemy positions clearly,** with a number, a location and their health if you can, such as "two on B, one hurt".
- **Say what you are doing,** such as "flashing in" or "smoking mid".
- **Share economy information,** such as "I can drop a rifle".
- **Stay calm.** Short, clear calls help much more than long explanations.

If you do not use voice chat, use the radio and ping options provided in the game. The aim is the same.

## Practicing with a partner

Practice is more effective and more fun with someone else.

1. **Take turns being the target.** One player holds an angle while the other practices peeking, flashing or smoking.
2. **Rehearse executes.** Walk through a site take with grenades and timings, and repeat it until it is smooth.
3. **Review together.** Watch a short clip and ask what each of you could do differently.
4. **Share lineups.** Show a teammate the throw and the position, and ask them to repeat it.

A private server makes this easy, and a friend gives honest feedback.

## Avoiding burnout in competitive practice

A game as demanding as Counter-Strike can be draining. Keep the habit healthy.

- **Keep practice short,** and stop when focus drops.
- **Play some relaxed matches** where you do not worry about the result.
- **Take a day off each week.**
- **Look after your hands, eyes and posture,** using our [desk setup guide](/gaming-gear/how-to-set-up-a-gaming-desk/) as a reference.

Steady, fun practice builds more skill over a year than bursts of intense grinding.

## Key takeaways

- Use a local practice server with bots to try recoil, movement and grenades without pressure.
- Stop before you shoot, learn the first few bullets of each rifle spray and fire bursts at range.
- Learn a few reliable smokes, flashes and molotovs for one map before you add more.
- Understand the economy and buy and save together with your team.
- Keep sessions short, set one measurable goal and review a match each week.

Counter-Strike rewards patience and small, repeatable habits. A short routine you keep for a month will do more than a long session you never repeat.
