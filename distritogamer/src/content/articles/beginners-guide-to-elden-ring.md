*Elden Ring* has a reputation for being punishing, but it is also one of the most welcoming open-world action games for players who know a few key ideas. FromSoftware's game, published by Bandai Namco in 2022 and available on PC, PlayStation and Xbox, gives you a huge map and a lot of freedom. The trouble is that it rarely explains itself, and beginners tend to learn the hard way.

This guide explains the systems that matter most in your first hours: how to choose a class, how to level up, how stats and equip load work, how to use your tools and how to approach bosses. For general habits, see our guide to [common mistakes new players make](/beginner-guides/common-mistakes-new-players-make/) and our [complete gaming tips guide](/#pillar). A reference summary of the game is available on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elden_Ring).

> **Note:** This guide explains systems and decisions rather than listing every item location. Patches may change numbers, so we avoid fragile details.

## Choosing a starting class

You pick a class at the start, but classes only set your **starting stats and equipment**, not your destiny. You can change your build later by leveling any attribute and using different weapons.

| Class | Why beginners like it | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Vagabond | High health, good armor, solid melee weapon and shield | Less ranged or magic flexibility early |
| Samurai | Strong katana and a bow for ranged attacks | A bit more stat-hungry later |
| Astrologer | Starts with sorceries for ranged damage | Lower health and defense; requires care |
| Wretch | Everything at level one for a self-built character | Hardest start; not recommended for first-timers |

If you want a simple recommendation, the Vagabond is forgiving, and the Samurai gives you a ranged option from the start.

## The first steps

You begin in a short opening area and then reach the Lands Between, starting in Limgrave. Some early principles:

- **Explore instead of rushing.** The game is open, and areas outside the main path hold weapons, items and experience.
- **Follow the Guidance of Grace.** The golden light you see points toward the next suggested direction, but you are not forced to follow it.
- **Meet the NPCs.** Early characters give you key tools, including the means to summon Spirit Ashes and to ride your horse-like steed.

## Sites of Grace, runes and leveling

**Sites of Grace** are the game's rest points. At a Site of Grace you can:

- Level up by spending runes
- Refill your flasks
- Fast-travel in many cases
- Manage equipment and your memorized spells

Resting also respawns most regular enemies, so it works like a reset.

**Runes** are both money and experience. You earn them from defeating enemies and spend them to level up and buy items. When you die, you drop all runes you were carrying, and you can recover them from your bloodstain if you reach it again. Because of this:

1. **Spend runes often.** Do not bank huge amounts before a hard fight.
2. **Do not panic on death.** Return to your bloodstain carefully.
3. **Level up regularly.** A few levels in the right stat make early areas much easier.

## Attributes: what to level first

Elden Ring has eight attributes: Vigor, Mind, Endurance, Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence, Faith and Arcane. A simple approach for beginners:

- **Vigor (health):** a good first priority. More health forgives mistakes.
- **Endurance (stamina and equip load):** helps you attack and dodge more and carry heavier gear.
- **Mind (focus points):** needed if you cast spells or use many weapon skills.
- **Your main damage stat:** Strength or Dexterity for most melee weapons, Intelligence or Faith for spells and incantations, and Arcane for some special builds.

Weapons list their attribute requirements, so check what a weapon needs before investing. A balanced early investment in Vigor and your damage stat is better than spreading points everywhere.

## Equip load and rolling

Your equipment weighs something, and your **equip load** is the share of your carrying capacity that you use. It changes how your dodge roll feels:

| Equip load | Roll type |
|---|---|
| Below about 30 percent | Fast roll |
| Below about 70 percent | Medium roll |
| Below about 100 percent | Heavy roll |

A fast or medium roll gives much better dodging, so many beginners aim for the medium roll and trade heavy armor for mobility. Heavier armor offers more protection but slows you down. This is one of the best places to experiment.

## Combat basics

- **Stamina is everything.** Attacking, rolling and blocking all use stamina. If you run out, you cannot act, so leave enough to dodge.
- **Lock on** with the lock-on button to track enemies, and switch targets when needed.
- **Learn enemy patterns.** Most enemies have repeating attacks. Watch them once or twice before you commit.
- **Attack after, not during.** The safest time to strike is right after an enemy finishes an attack.
- **Use your shield or dodge,** depending on your build. Guarding can reduce damage but drains stamina.
- **Heal when it is safe.** Drinking a flask in the middle of an enemy's combo is risky.

## Your tools

### Torrent

You get a spectral steed early in the game. Torrent makes traveling across Limgrave and beyond much faster, and you can fight from its back in open areas.

### Spirit Ashes

Spirit Ashes let you summon allies in many boss fights and some areas. They distract enemies and deal damage, which makes tough fights more forgiving. Upgrading them requires resources found as you explore.

### Flasks

You carry flasks that restore health and focus points. You can increase their number and strength by collecting items and visiting certain locations, so exploring is also a way to get stronger.

### Weapons and upgrades

Find a weapon you enjoy and upgrade it steadily. A well-upgraded weapon often beats a pile of unused ones. Upgrade materials come from exploration, so do not hoard them forever.

## Dealing with early bosses

Some bosses stop many beginners. The principles are always the same:

1. **Learn the patterns.** Fight, die, remember.
2. **Level up and upgrade your weapon,** then try again.
3. **Use Spirit Ashes** if the boss allows it.
4. **Try a different approach:** a ranged option, a shield or a different weapon.
5. **Leave and explore.** The game is open, and you are not required to beat every boss now.

Leaving and returning stronger is not a failure. It is the game's design.

## Exploration tips

- **Map fragments** reveal the map as you find them.
- **Checkpoint caution:** enemies respawn when you rest, so you can farm runes carefully if needed.
- **Optional areas** such as catacombs, caves and mines have rewards and bosses.
- **Do not fear death.** Each death teaches something, and you only lose unspent runes.

## Multiplayer and expansion

*Elden Ring* has optional co-operative and competitive multiplayer features. You can play the whole game alone, and many beginners finish their first run solo or with a friend. The *Shadow of the Erdtree* expansion adds a large new area for late-game characters, so do not start it until you are well-prepared.

## A first-session checklist

1. Pick a class, such as Vagabond or Samurai.
2. Explore Limgrave instead of rushing a boss.
3. Rest at Sites of Grace and level up Vigor first.
4. Check your equip load and aim for a medium roll.
5. Find a weapon you like and upgrade it.
6. Use Torrent and Spirit Ashes.
7. If a boss stops you, explore elsewhere and come back stronger.

## Mistakes to avoid in your first hours

Even with the basics, a few mistakes are common.

- **Fighting the first strong boss over and over.** If you are stuck, leave and explore. The game is built to allow it.
- **Hoarding runes.** Spend them, or you risk losing a large amount.
- **Ignoring defense and equip load.** A strong weapon does not help if you cannot dodge.
- **Spreading points across too many stats.** Choose a direction and invest in it.
- **Forgetting to upgrade your weapon.** An upgraded weapon often matters more than a few levels.
- **Skipping the options menu.** Camera, target lock and control options can make combat much more comfortable.

## Playing at your own pace

*Elden Ring* does not require you to follow a specific route. Some players prefer to explore thoroughly, while others follow the main path. Both are valid. If you feel overwhelmed, pick a direction, such as exploring the nearby caves and ruins before moving on, and keep a note of places you want to return to. Taking a break when you are frustrated, and coming back with fresh eyes, often solves a problem that hours of effort did not.

## Key takeaways

- Rest at Sites of Grace to level up, and spend runes instead of hoarding them.
- Put points into Vigor early, then into the stats your weapon needs.
- Keep equip load low enough for a fast or medium roll, since dodging is your main defense.
- Use Torrent, Spirit Ashes and your flasks, and upgrade one weapon steadily.
- When a boss stops you, explore somewhere else and return stronger.

Elden Ring is generous with freedom and stingy with explanations. Take your time, learn the patterns and treat every death as information rather than failure.
