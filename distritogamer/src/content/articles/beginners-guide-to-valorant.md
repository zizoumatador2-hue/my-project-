Valorant is a free-to-play tactical shooter from Riot Games, and it is easy to enjoy and hard to master. Two teams of five take turns attacking and defending, and every round matters. Your first matches can feel overwhelming because you must learn aim, abilities, maps and the economy at the same time.

This guide gives you a clear order to learn things in, so that you improve quickly and enjoy the process. For general improvement habits, see our [complete gaming tips guide](/#pillar) and [how to get better at gaming](/gaming-tips/how-to-get-better-at-gaming/). For a quick reference on the game, see [Valorant on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valorant).

> **Note:** Valorant is updated regularly. Agents, maps, weapon prices and modes change, so this guide focuses on principles that stay useful. Check the in-game information for current details.

## How a match works

In the standard mode, two teams of five play rounds as attackers or defenders:

- **Attackers** try to plant a device called the **Spike** at a bomb site, or eliminate all defenders.
- **Defenders** try to stop the plant, defuse the Spike after it is planted, or eliminate all attackers.
- **Sides swap** halfway through the match, and the first team to reach the required number of round wins takes the game.
- **Everyone has one life per round.** If you die, you wait until the next round.

Because you cannot respawn, information and teamwork are as important as aim. Valorant belongs to the [tactical shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_shooter) genre, where careful play beats reckless play.

## Modes to try first

Start in lower-pressure modes before competitive play. Valorant offers a standard unrated mode that uses the same rules without ranks, plus faster and casual modes. Competitive mode unlocks after you reach a certain account level. Playing unrated first helps you learn maps, agents and the economy without worrying about a rank.

## Agents and roles

Each agent has abilities in addition to guns. Agents belong to four roles:

| Role | Job | What you will practice |
|---|---|---|
| Duelist | Create entry and take space | Aggressive timing, aim, trading with teammates |
| Initiator | Gather information and set up entries | Using abilities to scout and disrupt |
| Controller | Block vision and split sites | Smoke placement and map control |
| Sentinel | Hold sites and watch flanks | Defensive positioning and information |

As a beginner:

1. **Pick one agent** and stay with it for many matches.
2. **Learn that agent's abilities** in the practice range, including how long they last and how they interact.
3. **Choose a role the team needs** if your first choice is already taken.
4. **Do not copy a complicated agent just because it looks cool.** Abilities that require precise lineups are harder than abilities that give direct information.

## The economy

Valorant has a buy phase at the start of each round. You earn credits based on round results and kills, and spend them on weapons, armor and abilities. Understanding the economy is a big step forward.

- **Full buy:** a rifle or similar primary weapon, armor and abilities.
- **Eco round:** save credits by buying little, so the next round can be a full buy.
- **Force buy:** spend what you have on the best available setup, usually when the opponent is likely to be rich.
- **Half buy:** a compromise, usually affordable but not full.

The golden rule is to **buy together and save together**. If four teammates buy and one cannot afford to, the team has a weak link, so coordinate. If someone can drop a weapon for a teammate, say so in team chat. Learn the pistol round, which every team plays at the start of each half with equal credits.

## The mechanics to learn first

Beginners often focus on flashy aim, but a few simple mechanics matter more:

1. **Crosshair placement.** Keep your crosshair at head height and aimed where an opponent will appear. See [how to improve aim in FPS games](/gaming-tips/how-to-improve-aim-in-fps-games/).
2. **Counter-strafing.** In Valorant, your first shot is most accurate when you are standing still. After moving, tap the opposite movement key briefly to stop, then shoot. This simple habit makes your shots much more accurate than shooting while moving.
3. **Walking quietly.** Holding the walk key makes your footsteps quiet, so enemies cannot hear you coming. Use it when sound matters.
4. **Controlled shooting.** Bursts and taps are more accurate than holding the trigger at long range. Learn your favorite weapon's behavior in the Range.
5. **Peeking carefully.** Clear angles one at a time, and avoid peeking with a long unprotected sprint.

## Practice before you play

Use the **Range** (the practice area) before matches:

- Shoot bots with your favorite weapons
- Practice counter-strafing and burst shooting
- Test your agent's abilities and learn how they work
- Try a custom game with friends to practice site executes

## Settings to check

Spend a few minutes in the settings menu:

- **Sensitivity:** keep it comfortable and stable. See [best sensitivity settings for gaming](/game-settings/best-sensitivity-settings-for-gaming/).
- **Crosshair:** choose a clear, high-contrast crosshair. You can customize it in the settings.
- **Minimap:** keep it visible and learn to glance at it.
- **Audio:** use stereo headphones if you can, and keep game sound clearer than music. Our [headset guide](/gaming-gear/best-gaming-headset-guide/) explains what to look for.
- **Frame rate and graphics:** aim for a stable, high frame rate. See [best FPS settings](/game-settings/best-fps-settings-for-better-performance/).

> **Note:** On Windows, Valorant uses an anti-cheat program that runs at system startup, and some Windows 11 setups require specific security features to be enabled. Follow the game's own prompts and Riot's support pages if you see an error.

## Communication and teamwork

Valorant rewards teams that share information:

- **Call what you see:** "Two A main, one low" is enough.
- **Use pings** if you do not use voice chat.
- **Stay near teammates** so that you can trade kills, meaning that one dies and another immediately eliminates the killer.
- **Be positive.** Blaming teammates makes everyone play worse.

## A simple plan for your first week

1. **Day 1 to 2:** unrated matches with one agent, focusing on learning maps and abilities.
2. **Day 3 to 4:** add 10 minutes in the Range before each session, focused on counter-strafing.
3. **Day 5:** review one match, and note your most common death. See [how to review your gameplay](/advanced-strategies/how-to-review-your-gameplay/).
4. **Day 6 to 7:** try one new idea, such as holding a defensive angle or buying with the team.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Shooting while running.** It is inaccurate; stop first.
- **Buying alone.** Coordinate with the team.
- **Changing agents constantly.** Learn one first.
- **Peeking without information.** Use abilities or teammates.
- **Ignoring sound.** Footsteps tell you where enemies are.
- **Tilting after a loss.** Take a short break.

## Reading the map and learning angles

Maps in a tactical shooter are as important as aim. You do not need to memorize everything at once, but a few habits help.

- **Learn the names of key areas** so that you and your team can talk about them. Most maps have callouts that appear on the map screen.
- **Learn where enemies can appear from** at each moment, and place your crosshair there.
- **Learn common paths,** including the long routes players take to flank.
- **Learn which sound cues matter,** such as the sound of the Spike being planted or defused.
- **Study one map at a time** by playing it several times, so that you are not overwhelmed.

Use a custom game with friends or bots to walk around a map without time pressure. Look at common angles and ask what you can see from each.

## Playing attack and defense

The two sides have different jobs, and a little understanding goes a long way.

**On attack:**

- Decide a plan with the team before the round, even if it is simple.
- Use abilities to gain information and cover before entering a site.
- Plant in a position you can defend, and think about where defenders will retake from.
- After planting, play for time, because the Spike counting down helps you.

**On defense:**

- Hold angles patiently and avoid unnecessary pushes.
- Use abilities to delay and gather information.
- Rotate to help a site only when you have good information.
- After a plant, coordinate a retake instead of arriving one by one.

## Dealing with a bad streak

Everyone has losing streaks, and a few habits help you recover.

1. **Take a break** after two or three losses.
2. **Switch to a lower-pressure mode,** such as unrated, for a match or two.
3. **Check your setup** for new issues such as a dropped frame rate.
4. **Review one loss** and find one pattern.
5. **Return with a single goal,** such as holding an angle patiently.

A streak is a sign to slow down, not to play more.

## Key takeaways

- Attackers plant the Spike and defenders stop them, and every player has one life per round.
- Pick one agent, learn its abilities in the Range and stay with it for many matches.
- Buy together and save together, because the economy decides most rounds before a shot is fired.
- Stop before you shoot, keep your crosshair at head height and use footsteps and information.
- Review a loss each week and pick one habit to change.

Valorant has a steep learning curve, but a few habits do most of the work. Start with those, and the rest will make more sense each week.
