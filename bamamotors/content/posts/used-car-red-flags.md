**Quick answer:** The biggest used car red flags are a seller who won't allow an independent inspection, a title brand (salvage, rebuilt or flood) you weren't told about, mileage that doesn't match the records, signs of flood damage, and pressure to pay before you've seen the car. Any one of them is a reason to slow down. Several are a reason to walk away.

## Paperwork red flags

1. **Title brands:** Salvage, rebuilt, flood or lemon. Check through [NMVTIS](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/).
2. **Name mismatch:** The seller's ID doesn't match the title.
3. **Missing or "lost" title:** A common sign of a problem sale.
4. **Mileage inconsistencies** between the odometer, history report and service records.

## Condition red flags

5. **Flood signs:** Musty odor, silt under carpets, rust on seat rails. Especially relevant after Gulf Coast storms.
6. **Mismatched paint or panel gaps:** Evidence of accident repair.
7. **Warning lights** on the dash, or none at all at key-on (bulbs removed?).
8. **Fresh undercoating** on an older car, which can hide rust.

## Seller behavior red flags

9. **No inspection allowed:** The number one warning sign.
10. **Pressure tactics:** "Another buyer is coming in an hour."
11. **Deposit before viewing:** Never pay for a car you haven't seen.
12. **Price far below market:** Compare with local listings on our city pages, such as [Birmingham](/used-cars/birmingham-al) or [Mobile](/used-cars/mobile-al).

## What to do instead

- Get an itemized **out-the-door price** in writing.
