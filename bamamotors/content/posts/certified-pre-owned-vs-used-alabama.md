**Quick answer:** A certified pre-owned (CPO) car is a late-model used vehicle that passed a manufacturer or dealer inspection and usually includes an extended warranty. It costs more than a comparable used car, so it's worth it if you want warranty coverage, especially on vehicles that are expensive to repair.

## What CPO usually includes

- A multi-point **inspection** and reconditioning
- An **extended warranty** beyond the original factory coverage
- Often **roadside assistance** and a vehicle history report
- Sometimes special **financing rates** from the manufacturer

## Manufacturer vs. dealer certification

| | Manufacturer CPO | Dealer "certified" |
|---|---|---|
| Who sets the standards | The automaker | The individual dealer |
| Warranty backed by | The manufacturer | The dealer or a third party |
| Consistency | Same program nationwide | Varies widely |

Always ask: **who backs the warranty, what does it cover, and for how long?** Get it in writing.

## When CPO is worth it

- You're buying a **luxury or complex vehicle** with costly repairs.
- You want **predictable costs** for the next few years.
- You plan to keep the car past its original factory warranty.

## When a regular used car makes more sense

- You're buying an **older or budget** vehicle. See [cars under $10,000](/blog/cars-under-10000-in-alabama).
- The model has a strong reliability record and complete service history.
- You'd rather keep the price difference for maintenance.

## Don't skip your own checks

Even certified cars should get an [independent inspection](/blog/how-to-inspect-a-used-car-alabama). Review the history, confirm open recalls were completed, and read the Buyers Guide on the window.
