**Quick answer:** To finance a used car in Alabama, check your credit, get pre-approved by a bank or credit union, choose the shortest loan term you can comfortably afford, make a meaningful down payment, and compare the dealer's financing offer against your pre-approval using the APR and total cost — not just the monthly payment.

## Step 1: Know your credit

Your credit score is the biggest factor in the interest rate you'll be offered. You can get free credit reports from each of the three bureaus at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/). Fix errors before you apply — even a small score improvement can lower your rate.

## Step 2: Get pre-approved

A pre-approval from a bank or credit union tells you your maximum loan amount and approximate APR. Alabama has many local credit unions that offer competitive auto loans to members. Walking into a dealership pre-approved gives you a firm benchmark.

## Step 3: Decide on a down payment and term

- **Down payment:** A larger down payment reduces the amount you borrow and helps you avoid being "upside down" (owing more than the car is worth).
- **Loan term:** 36-, 48- and 60-month loans are common for used cars. Longer terms reduce the monthly payment but increase total interest.

### Payment comparison

The table below shows how term and rate change the monthly payment on a $20,000 loan. These are illustrative calculations, not offers.

| Loan amount | APR | Term | Est. monthly payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| $20,000 | 6% | 48 months | about $470 |
| $20,000 | 6% | 60 months | about $387 |
| $20,000 | 10% | 60 months | about $425 |
| $20,000 | 14% | 72 months | about $412 |

Notice that the 72-month loan at 14% has a lower payment than the 60-month loan at 10%, but costs far more in total interest.

## Step 4: Compare dealer financing

Dealers can arrange financing through multiple lenders. Ask for the **APR, term and total amount financed** in writing and compare them against your pre-approval. The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's auto loan resources](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/auto-loans/) explain what to look for in a loan contract.

## Step 5: Read the contract

Before you sign, confirm the price, APR, term, down payment and any add-ons match what you agreed to. Optional products such as GAP insurance and service contracts should be itemized — and you can decline them.
