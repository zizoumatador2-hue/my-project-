**Quick answer:** Inspect a used car in six steps: walk around the exterior, inspect the interior, look under the hood, check for flood damage, take a thorough test drive, and finish with an independent mechanic's inspection. In Alabama — where there's no statewide safety inspection — that last step is especially important.

## 1. Exterior walk-around

- Uneven panel gaps or mismatched paint can indicate prior accident repair.
- Look underneath for rust on the frame, brake lines and exhaust.
- Check **tire tread depth** and **tire age** — the last four digits of the DOT code show the week and year of manufacture. Tires older than about six years may need replacing regardless of tread.

## 2. Interior

- Turn the key to "on" and confirm every warning light illuminates and then goes out once the engine starts.
- Test windows, locks, mirrors, seats, infotainment, Bluetooth and USB ports.
- Run the **A/C at full blast** for several minutes. In Alabama, weak A/C is a costly surprise.

## 3. Under the hood

- Engine oil should be amber to dark brown, not milky.
- Transmission fluid (where checkable) shouldn't smell burnt.
- Look for leaks, cracked hoses and corroded battery terminals.

## 4. Flood damage signs

After Gulf Coast hurricanes and heavy storms, flood-damaged vehicles can reach the market. Watch for:

- A **musty smell**, or heavy air freshener covering one
- **New carpet** in an older car, or damp carpet and padding
- **Silt, sand or water lines** under seats, in the spare-tire well or behind panels
- **Rust on seat rails**, screws and pedal brackets
- **Fogged headlights**, taillights or gauge clusters

Always check the title history through [NMVTIS](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/) for flood or salvage brands.

## 5. Test drive

Drive at least 20 minutes on both local roads and the highway. Brake firmly, check that the car tracks straight, listen for clunks over bumps and feel for transmission hesitation.

## 6. Independent inspection

Ask a trusted mechanic to inspect the car on a lift. A reputable dealer will allow it. You can also check for open safety recalls on [NHTSA.gov](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).
