Romantic getaways are short or long trips planned around one goal: spending real, unhurried time together. That can mean a two-night escape to a Hudson Valley inn, a week at an adults-only resort in Mexico, or a honeymoon on Maui. What they share is a feeling of being somewhere special with the person you love, without the noise of everyday life.

This guide pulls together everything our editors have learned about planning the best romantic getaways for couples in the United States, Mexico and the Caribbean. We cover how to choose a destination, how to match a trip to your budget and calendar, when adults-only and all-inclusive resorts make sense, and the small details that turn a nice trip into one you'll talk about for years. Every section links to a deeper guide, so you can go as far down the rabbit hole as you like.

## What makes a great romantic getaway?

After reviewing dozens of destinations and resorts, we keep coming back to five ingredients. The more of them a trip has, the more romantic it feels.

1. **Easy logistics.** Stress kills romance. Short transfers, simple itineraries and a comfortable base let you relax from the first evening.
2. **Privacy.** A balcony with a view, a quiet pool or a secluded beach gives you space to talk, read and reconnect.
3. **Beauty.** Sunsets over the water, vineyards at golden hour, mountains in the mist. Scenery does a lot of the emotional heavy lifting.
4. **A shared experience.** One memorable activity, such as a sunset sail, a couples massage or a sunrise hike, gives the trip a story.
5. **Great food.** A long dinner with no phones on the table is still the most romantic thing most couples do on vacation.

Notice what's missing: a packed schedule. The most common mistake we see is trying to do too much. A romantic getaway works best with one or two plans per day and plenty of room for spontaneity.

## How to choose the right romantic getaway

The right destination depends on four things: how much time you have, what you want to spend, when you're traveling and the vibe you both want. Work through them in that order and the choice usually becomes obvious.

### Start with your time

Time is the biggest filter because travel days eat into the trip.

- **Two or three nights:** choose a place you can reach in about three hours or less, door to door. Think of drive-to inns, short flights to Miami or Las Vegas, or a quick hop to the Florida Keys. Our guide to [romantic weekend getaways for couples](/guides/weekend-getaways-for-couples/) is built around this rule.
- **Four or five nights:** a nonstop flight to Mexico, the Bahamas or Jamaica works well. Many major airports in the eastern and central U.S. have nonstop flights to Cancun.
- **A week or more:** now it's worth crossing time zones for Hawaii or combining two places, such as Miami and the Keys, or Maui and Kauai.

### Then set a budget you both feel good about

Money conversations aren't very romantic, but having one before you book prevents stress later. Decide on a total number for the trip, including flights, lodging, food, activities and tips. Then decide what matters most to you as a couple: a better room, better dinners or more experiences. Our guides to [affordable romantic getaways](/guides/affordable-romantic-getaways/) and [the best couples vacations under $2,000](/guides/best-couples-vacations-under-2000/) show how to make a modest budget feel generous.

### Pick the right season

Weather and crowds change everything. The Caribbean and Mexico are driest from December through April, which is also when prices peak. Hurricane season in the Atlantic runs June 1 through November 30. Hawaii is pleasant year-round, with quieter shoulder seasons in spring and fall. The mainland U.S. offers something every month, from fall foliage in New England to spring wildflowers in California.

### Agree on the vibe

Finally, talk about what kind of trip you both want. Beach and do nothing? Food and wine? Adventure? City lights? Use the table below as a starting point.

## Types of romantic getaways

Romantic getaways come in a few distinct styles. Most couples know right away which one sounds like them.

### Beach escapes

The classic. Warm water, soft sand and nothing on the agenda except the next swim. The U.S. has more great beaches than most people realize, from Siesta Key to Maui's Wailea. Our list of the [best beach vacations](/guides/best-beach-vacations/) compares the top options, and we have dedicated guides to the [best beach vacations in the USA](/guides/best-beach-vacations-in-the-usa/) and the [best beach vacations in the Caribbean](/guides/best-beach-vacations-in-the-caribbean/).

### Adults-only and all-inclusive resorts

For many couples, the most relaxing trip is one where you never reach for your wallet and never hear a kids' pool party. Adults-only all-inclusive resorts in Mexico, Jamaica and the Dominican Republic are designed exactly for that. We explain how they work in the next chapter.

### Weekend getaways

Short trips close to home are the most underrated romantic getaways. They're easier to schedule, cheaper and perfect for anniversaries or a reset between big trips. See [romantic weekend getaways from New York](/guides/best-weekend-getaways-from-new-york/) and [Florida weekend getaways](/guides/florida-weekend-getaways/) for ready-made plans.

### City breaks

Cities can be surprisingly romantic: candlelit restaurants, rooftop bars, museums and shows. New York, Miami and Las Vegas each offer a different flavor. Our [Las Vegas couples vacation](/guides/las-vegas-couples-vacation/) guide shows how to find the romance beyond the casino floor.

### Wine country and scenic drives

Napa Valley, Sonoma and Big Sur are made for slow travel. Book a beautiful inn, plan one tasting per day and leave time to wander. See our [romantic getaways in the USA](/guides/romantic-getaways-in-the-usa/) for the best routes.

### Honeymoons and milestone trips

Honeymoons, big anniversaries and "we finally have time" trips deserve a little extra. Our [honeymoon destinations](/guides/honeymoon-destinations/) and [best honeymoon resorts](/guides/best-honeymoon-resorts/) guides focus on places that deliver unforgettable settings and service.

### Luxury escapes

Sometimes the splurge is the point. Private plunge pools, butler service and rooms with no fourth wall are real, and they're wonderful. ## Romantic getaways by season

Every season has its own kind of romance. Matching your trip to the calendar is one of the easiest ways to get better weather, fewer crowds and better value.

### Spring (March to May)

Spring is prime time for many romantic getaways. Florida's humidity hasn't arrived yet, Hawaii is between its winter peak and summer family season, and California's hills turn green with wildflowers. Late April and May can also be a sweet spot for Caribbean and Mexican resorts, once the Easter rush ends and before hurricane season begins. Avoid spring-break weeks in beach towns if you want quiet.

**Spring ideas:** Napa Valley before harvest crowds, the Florida Keys in late April, an adults-only resort in the Riviera Maya in May, or a garden-filled weekend in Savannah or Charleston.

### Summer (June to August)

Summer is family season in many destinations, so couples do best by going where kids aren't, or by going somewhere cooler. Adults-only resorts come into their own. Mountain and lake getaways, New England coastal towns and the Pacific Northwest shine. Summer is also low season in parts of Mexico and the Caribbean, which can mean value, with the trade-offs of heat, humidity and the start of hurricane season.

**Summer ideas:** a Hudson Valley or Catskills weekend, Cape Cod or Newport, a Lake Tahoe cabin, or a flexible-rate adults-only escape in Mexico.

### Fall (September to November)

Fall is our favorite season for couples. Summer crowds disappear, many places are still warm, and prices often ease. Hawaii's fall shoulder season is a gem. Big Sur often has clearer skies than in summer. The Northeast bursts into color, making it the classic time for a New England or Hudson Valley getaway. It's also the heart of harvest season in wine country.

**Fall ideas:** a Maui honeymoon in October, foliage in Vermont or the Catskills, harvest-season Sonoma, or Las Vegas once the desert heat breaks.

### Winter (December to February)

Winter is when warm-weather escapes are most tempting, and most popular. The Caribbean, Mexico, Miami and the Florida Keys enjoy their best weather, so book early. Hawaii's winter brings humpback whales and big surf on north shores. If you'd rather embrace the cold, a snowy mountain lodge with a fireplace is its own kind of romantic.

**Winter ideas:** a St. Lucia hideaway over the holidays, Miami's dry season, whale watching in Maui or Los Cabos, or a cozy ski-town cabin.

## Romantic getaways for every milestone

Different occasions call for different trips. Here's how we'd approach the big ones.

- **Honeymoon.** Go big on setting and service, and give yourselves at least a week if you can. Hawaii, St. Lucia and adults-only resorts in Mexico are perennial favorites. Start with our [honeymoon destinations](/guides/honeymoon-destinations/) guide.
- **First anniversary.** Recreate a favorite memory or plan a "first": your first trip abroad together, your first wine-country weekend or your first time snorkeling.
- **Milestone anniversary.** This is the moment for a bucket-list splurge, like a suite with a private plunge pool or a clifftop hotel in Big Sur. See [luxury resorts for couples](/guides/luxury-resorts-for-couples/).
- **Proposal trip.** Choose a place with a naturally beautiful, uncrowded spot at golden hour, and scout it the day before.
- **Babymoon.** Short flights, great food and a comfortable resort beat an ambitious itinerary. Check any health-related travel advice with your doctor first.
- **Just because.** A spontaneous weekend often becomes the trip you remember most. Our [romantic weekend getaways](/guides/weekend-getaways-for-couples/) make it easy.
