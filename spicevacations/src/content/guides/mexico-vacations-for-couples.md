Mexico is one of the easiest and most rewarding destinations for American couples. Many flights are short and nonstop, the beaches are beautiful, the food is extraordinary, and the country has one of the world's largest collections of adults-only all-inclusive resorts. Beyond the beach, you'll find Mayan ruins, freshwater cenotes, colonial towns and vibrant cities.

This guide compares the best regions for Mexico vacations for couples, with where to stay and when to go. For adults-only resort picks, see [the best adults-only resorts in Mexico](/guides/best-adults-only-resorts-in-mexico/), and for broader planning, our complete [romantic getaways](/) guide.

## Mexico's Caribbean coast

### Cancun and Playa Mujeres

Cancun's Hotel Zone is a long strip of resorts between the Caribbean and a lagoon, with nightlife, restaurants and shopping. It's convenient and lively. Just north, Playa Mujeres is quieter, with spacious adults-only resorts like [Excellence Playa Mujeres](/resorts/excellence-playa-mujeres/). In the Hotel Zone, [Le Blanc Spa Resort Cancun](/resorts/le-blanc-spa-resort-cancun/) is known for butler service and its spa.

### The Riviera Maya

South of Cancun, the Riviera Maya stretches past Puerto Morelos and Playa del Carmen toward Tulum. Maroma Beach is one of its most admired beaches, home to [Secrets Maroma Beach](/resorts/secrets-maroma-beach/). In Playa del Carmen, [Hotel Xcaret Arte](/resorts/hotel-xcaret-arte/) celebrates Mexican art and culture.

### Tulum

Tulum is known for its boho-chic boutique hotels, beach clubs and the Mayan ruins perched on cliffs above the sea. It's stylish and relaxed, with excellent restaurants. Tulum's international airport has made it easier to reach.

### Isla Mujeres and Cozumel

Isla Mujeres, a short ferry from Cancun, has calm, shallow water at Playa Norte and a relaxed island pace. Cozumel, reached by ferry from Playa del Carmen, is famous for diving and snorkeling on its reefs.

## Mexico's Pacific coast

### Los Cabos

At the southern tip of Baja California, Los Cabos combines desert mountains with the Pacific Ocean. Cabo San Lucas is lively, with a marina and nightlife; San José del Cabo is quieter and more artsy; the Tourist Corridor between them has luxurious resorts. Take a boat to El Arco, the famous rock arch, and look for humpback whales in winter. Many Cabo beaches are too rough for swimming, but Medano Beach, home to [Breathless Cabo San Lucas](/resorts/breathless-cabo-san-lucas/), is one of the more swimmable ones.

### Puerto Vallarta

Puerto Vallarta has a walkable old town with cobblestone streets, a seaside malecon lined with sculptures and jungle-covered hills rising behind the Bay of Banderas. It's great for couples who want culture and good food along with the beach.

## Beyond the beach: Mexico City

Mexico City is one of the world's great food and culture capitals. Explore the historic center, the museums of Chapultepec Park, the leafy neighborhoods of Roma and Condesa, and the colorful canals of Xochimilco. It pairs beautifully with a few beach days on either coast.

## Romantic things to do in Mexico

- **Swim in a cenote:** freshwater sinkholes in the Yucatán Peninsula.
- **Visit Mayan ruins** at Tulum, Cobá or Chichén Itzá.
- **Snorkel with sea turtles** at Akumal or on Cozumel's reefs.
- **Sail to El Arco** in Los Cabos.
- **Watch whales** in Los Cabos in winter.
- **Take a cooking class** and learn to make mole or tacos.
- **Book a couples temazcal** or spa treatment inspired by traditional rituals.

## Safety and practical tips

- **Check the State Department's travel advisory** for the specific Mexican state you'll visit.
- **Use authorized transportation** from the airport and your resort.
- **Carry a valid passport** and keep a copy separately.
- **Drink bottled or filtered water**; most resorts provide it.
- **Ask resorts about sargassum** on the Caribbean coast in warmer months.

## Sample itineraries

**Five nights, Riviera Maya:** an adults-only all-inclusive, with a cenote and Tulum ruins day trip.

**Seven nights, Los Cabos:** a resort in the Tourist Corridor, a sunset sail to El Arco, whale watching in winter and a dinner in San José del Cabo's art district.

**Eight nights, city and beach:** three nights in Mexico City for food and culture, then five nights on the Riviera Maya.

## Where to stay: all-inclusive vs. boutique

On the Caribbean coast, adults-only all-inclusives are the most popular choice for couples who want a relaxing, low-planning trip. In Tulum and Puerto Vallarta, boutique hotels and beach clubs offer more character and easy access to local restaurants. In Los Cabos, luxury resorts along the Tourist Corridor combine dramatic views with excellent dining, while Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo have smaller hotels closer to town.

## Mexican food to try together

Mexico's food is one of the best reasons to visit. On the Yucatán Peninsula, try cochinita pibil (slow-roasted pork), sopa de lima and fresh ceviche. In Baja, fish tacos and fresh seafood are a must. In Puerto Vallarta, sample raicilla, a local agave spirit, and street tacos. In Mexico City, explore markets, tacos al pastor, mole and some of the world's most celebrated restaurants. A cooking class is a fun activity for couples, and you get to bring the recipes home to recreate the trip in your own kitchen.

## Mexico on different budgets

**Value:** shoulder-season stays in Cancun and the Riviera Maya, Puerto Vallarta's old town and Mexico City's affordable food scene.

**Mid-range:** adults-only all-inclusives in Playa Mujeres and the Riviera Maya, and boutique hotels in Tulum.

**Splurge:** Los Cabos' luxury resorts and top-tier adults-only all-inclusives with butler service.

## Getting around

In Cancun and the Riviera Maya, pre-booked airport transfers and organized tours are easiest; some couples rent a car to explore cenotes and ruins independently. In Los Cabos, taxis and transfers connect the two towns and the corridor. In Mexico City, use authorized taxis or rideshares. Always use official transportation from airports.

## Mistakes to avoid in Mexico

1. **Booking a Pacific-side resort expecting calm Caribbean water.** Many Los Cabos beaches are not swimmable.
2. **Underestimating transfer times** to Playa Mujeres, Tulum or the Tourist Corridor.
3. **Ignoring the season.** Summer is hot and humid, and hurricane season lasts into November.
4. **Skipping the fine print** on all-inclusive inclusions and resort fees.
5. **Not venturing out.** A cenote, ruins or a local market adds a lot to your trip.

## Mexico for honeymooners

Mexico is one of the most popular honeymoon destinations for U.S. couples. The Caribbean coast's adults-only all-inclusives with swim-up suites make for an effortless honeymoon after a busy wedding, while Los Cabos offers luxury and dramatic scenery. Combine a few beach days with Mexico City or a cultural day trip for a well-rounded first trip as a married couple. See [honeymoon destinations](/guides/honeymoon-destinations/) for more.

## Our quick recommendations

For a first trip, choose an adults-only all-inclusive in **Playa Mujeres** or the **Riviera Maya**. For luxury and scenery, choose **Los Cabos**. For charm and culture, choose **Puerto Vallarta**. For food lovers, add a few nights in **Mexico City**. Whichever you pick, leave time for at least one adventure beyond the resort, like a cenote swim, a sail to El Arco or a walk along the malecon at sunset. Those moments outside the resort are often the ones couples remember most, long after the tan has faded and the photos have been shared.
