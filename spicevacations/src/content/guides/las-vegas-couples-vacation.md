Las Vegas has a reputation for bachelor parties and all-night gambling, but it can be one of the most fun and surprisingly romantic trips a couple takes. World-class shows, dinners with glittering views, lavish spas and red-rock desert landscapes are all within easy reach, and flights from most U.S. cities are frequent.

This guide shows how to plan a Las Vegas couples vacation that goes beyond the casino floor. For more U.S. ideas, see [romantic getaways in the USA](/guides/romantic-getaways-in-the-usa/) and our complete [romantic getaways](/) guide.

## Why Vegas works for couples

- **Entertainment every night:** Cirque du Soleil productions, residencies, comedy and magic.
- **Exceptional dining:** celebrity-chef restaurants, steakhouses and dinners with Strip views.
- **Spas and pools:** some of the largest resort spas in the country.
- **Desert adventures:** Red Rock Canyon, Valley of Fire and the Hoover Dam.
- **Easy logistics:** Harry Reid International Airport is just minutes from the Strip.

## Where to stay

### For classic romance: Bellagio

The [Bellagio](/resorts/bellagio/) is the Vegas resort people picture when they think of romance. Its dancing fountains, set to music, are one of the city's iconic free shows, and fountain-view rooms put you right above them. Don't miss the seasonal displays in the Conservatory & Botanical Gardens.

### For calm and privacy: Waldorf Astoria Las Vegas

The [Waldorf Astoria Las Vegas](/resorts/waldorf-astoria-las-vegas/) has no casino. You arrive at a 23rd-floor sky lobby with city views, and the atmosphere is hushed and elegant. It's walking distance to shows and restaurants, but it feels like a retreat.

### Other options

Many Strip resorts offer suites with floor-to-ceiling windows, adults-only pool areas and spas. Some off-Strip resorts are quieter and closer to the desert. Compare total prices, including resort fees.

## Romantic things to do in Las Vegas

### See a show

Book one or two shows in advance. Cirque du Soleil productions are spectacular, while intimate magic, comedy or music shows can feel more personal.

### Dinner with a view

Several Strip restaurants overlook the Bellagio fountains or the city lights. Reserve a table by the window for sunset.

### Watch the Bellagio fountains

The fountains perform regularly in the afternoon and evening. Watch from the lakeside promenade, then walk through the Conservatory.

### Ride the High Roller

The High Roller observation wheel at the LINQ offers sweeping views of the Strip, especially at night.

### Spend a day at the spa

Many resorts have large spas with thermal pools, steam rooms and couples treatment rooms. Book in advance, especially on weekends.

### Take a helicopter ride

Night helicopter tours over the Strip are dazzling, and daytime flights can take you to the Grand Canyon's West Rim.

### Explore Red Rock Canyon

About a 30-minute drive west of the Strip, Red Rock Canyon National Conservation Area has a scenic loop drive and hiking trails among red sandstone cliffs. Timed-entry reservations for the scenic drive are required during parts of the year, so check before you go.

### Visit Valley of Fire State Park

About an hour from the Strip, Nevada's oldest state park glows orange at sunrise and sunset, with dramatic rock formations and ancient petroglyphs.

### See Hoover Dam

About 45 minutes away, the Hoover Dam is an engineering marvel, with views over Lake Mead.

### Explore Fremont Street and the Arts District

Downtown Las Vegas has the Fremont Street Experience's light canopy, vintage neon and a creative Arts District with galleries and bars.

## A three-day Las Vegas itinerary for couples

### Day 1: Arrive and settle in

Check in, relax by the pool and watch the Bellagio fountains at sunset. Have dinner with a Strip view, then catch a show.

### Day 2: Desert and spa

Head to Red Rock Canyon early for a hike or scenic drive while temperatures are cool. Return for a spa afternoon. In the evening, dress up for a special dinner and cocktails.

### Day 3: Explore and celebrate

Visit Valley of Fire for sunrise or take a helicopter tour. In the afternoon, explore downtown's Arts District. End with a second show or a ride on the High Roller.

## Budget tips

- **Travel Sunday to Thursday** for lower hotel rates.
- **Watch for resort fees** when comparing hotels.
- **Enjoy free attractions:** the fountains, Conservatory, Fremont Street and people-watching.
- **Buy show tickets early** or look for same-day discounts.
- **Eat well at lunch**, when many restaurants are cheaper.

## Tips for a romantic Vegas trip

- **Plan around the heat** in summer; do outdoor activities early.
- **Stay hydrated** in the dry desert air.
- **Reserve dinners and shows** ahead of time.
- **Choose a high-floor room** for views.
- **Take a break from the Strip** with a desert day trip.

## Getting married or renewing vows

Las Vegas is famous for weddings and vow renewals, from chapels to scenic desert ceremonies. If you're planning one, check Clark County's marriage license requirements in advance.

## Romantic dining ideas

Las Vegas is one of America's great restaurant cities. For a special night, book a table with a view of the Strip or the Bellagio fountains, a chef's tasting menu or a classic steakhouse. For something more relaxed, try downtown's Arts District, where independent restaurants and cocktail bars feel more local. Many resorts also have lovely brunch spots for a slow morning. Reserve popular restaurants well ahead, especially for Friday and Saturday nights.

## Pool days for couples

From spring through early fall, pool time is a highlight. Many resorts have adults-only pool areas that are quieter than the main pools, and some rent cabanas or daybeds for a more private experience. Arrive early for the best loungers, bring plenty of sunscreen and water, and retreat indoors during the hottest afternoon hours in summer.

## A calmer side of Vegas

If you'd rather avoid crowds and noise, stay at a non-gaming hotel, spend mornings at the spa, take sunrise trips into the desert and choose intimate shows over big productions. Midweek visits are quieter than weekends, and the city is especially pleasant in spring and fall.

## Day trips beyond the city

With an extra day, consider a scenic drive to Zion National Park in southern Utah, about two and a half to three hours away, or a trip to the Grand Canyon's West Rim. Closer to town, Lake Mead offers boating and kayaking, and Mount Charleston's forests are much cooler than the city in summer.

## What to pack

Comfortable walking shoes (Strip distances are longer than they look), a dressy outfit for dinner and shows, a swimsuit, sunglasses, a hat and a light jacket for air-conditioned casinos and cool desert nights.

## Mistakes couples make in Vegas

1. **Staying too far from the action** to save money, then spending it on rideshares.
2. **Overbooking shows and dinners**, leaving no time to relax.
3. **Forgetting resort fees** when comparing hotel prices.
4. **Walking the Strip at midday in summer**; distances are long and heat is intense.
5. **Skipping the desert**, which is one of the most romantic parts of any Vegas trip.

## A romantic Vegas weekend on a budget

Stay midweek at a well-located Strip hotel, enjoy the free fountain show and Conservatory, picnic at Red Rock Canyon, and choose one show and one special dinner as your splurges. It's a surprisingly affordable way to have a glamorous weekend together.

## Vegas by season for couples

**Spring:** Ideal for hiking Red Rock and Valley of Fire, with warm pool days by May. **Summer:** Extremely hot; plan pool mornings, indoor afternoons and evening shows. **Fall:** Pleasant temperatures return, great for desert trips and outdoor dining. **Winter:** Cooler days, festive decorations and quieter weekdays, with occasional chilly nights.

## Where to watch the sunset

Watch from a Strip-view restaurant or bar, at Red Rock Canyon's overlooks, or from the High Roller as the city lights come on. In the desert, sunset colors can be spectacular, especially after a passing storm.

## Final tip

Treat Las Vegas like any great city break: choose a comfortable base, book the one or two experiences you care about most, and leave space for surprises. The best Vegas memories often come from an unplanned late-night walk down the Strip hand in hand, stopping to watch the fountains one more time.
