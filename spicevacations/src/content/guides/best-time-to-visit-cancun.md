Cancun is warm all year, so the real question is what you want to trade off: perfect weather and higher prices, or better value with more heat, rain or seaweed. This guide breaks it down month by month, with the two factors most first-timers miss: hurricane season and sargassum seaweed.

## Cancun by season at a glance

| Season | Months | Weather | Crowds and prices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dry season (peak) | December–April | Sunny, lower humidity, warm days and pleasant evenings | Highest, especially holidays and spring break |
| Shoulder | Late April–May, November | Hot in May, drying out in November | Moderate; often good value |
| Rainy and hurricane season | June–October | Hot and humid, short heavy showers; storm risk highest August–October | Lowest, except July and August school holidays |

## Month by month

| Month | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| January | Dry and sunny, with the coolest evenings of the year. Prices ease after New Year's week. |
| February | One of the most reliable months for weather. Valentine's and Presidents' Day weekends are busy. |
| March | Warm and dry. Spring break brings crowds and higher rates for much of the month. |
| April | Still dry and getting hotter. Easter week is busy; late April is a sweet spot. |
| May | Hot, before the rains start. Sargassum may start to appear. Good value. |
| June | Rainy season begins and hurricane season opens June 1. Humid, with afternoon showers. |
| July | Hot and humid; U.S. summer travel keeps family resorts busy. |
| August | Among the hottest months, and peak hurricane season begins. |
| September | Usually the wettest month and the quietest for crowds, with some of the lowest rates. |
| October | Rain eases late in the month, though storm risk continues. |
| November | Drier and more comfortable as the month goes on. A strong shoulder month outside Thanksgiving week. |
| December | Dry season returns. Christmas to New Year's is the most expensive week of the year. |

## Hurricane season: what it means for your trip

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30, and activity typically peaks from August through October. Most weeks pass without a storm affecting Cancun, but if one does, it can mean canceled flights or a few days indoors. If you travel in these months:

- Choose flexible or refundable bookings where possible.
- Consider travel insurance that covers weather, bought soon after you book.
- Follow forecasts from the U.S. National Hurricane Center in the week before you leave.

## Sargassum seaweed

Sargassum is a floating seaweed that washes up on many Caribbean beaches. In the Cancun area it tends to show up in spring and is usually heaviest in summer, but some years are much worse than others, and conditions change from week to week. A few practical points:

- Beaches facing the open Caribbean, including much of the Riviera Maya, tend to see more seaweed than sheltered areas such as Isla Mujeres and parts of Playa Mujeres.
- Many resorts rake their beaches each morning and use offshore barriers, which helps but does not solve the problem on heavy days.
- Mexico's federal authorities publish sargassum monitoring updates, and local news and recent traveler photos are a good real-time check.

If a seaweed-free beach matters most to you, the dry season months of December through March are usually the safest bet.

## Cancun or Riviera Maya?

The Cancun Hotel Zone is lively, close to the airport and lined with big resorts. Playa Mujeres, just north, is calmer. South of Cancun, the Riviera Maya toward Playa del Carmen and Tulum is more spread out, with smaller resorts like [Secrets Maroma Beach](/resorts/secrets-maroma-beach/) and longer airport transfers. ## Getting around Cancun and the Riviera Maya

Where you stay changes how much you will want to travel for sightseeing. Cancun's Hotel Zone is a long strip of resorts, restaurants and shopping, and the airport is usually a short taxi or shuttle ride away. The Riviera Maya, south toward Playa del Carmen and Tulum, is spread out, so a transfer from the airport can take noticeably longer. Plan your transport when you book, and ask your resort whether airport transfers are included.

Renting a car gives you flexibility for day trips, but driving conditions and parking vary, and some resorts are hard to reach without a taxi. If you plan to visit ruins or cenotes, ask your hotel about guided tours; many include transport, which removes the stress of navigating on your own.

## Day trips worth planning around the weather

Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula has far more to see than beaches. Chichén Itzá is one of the best-known Mayan sites, and it is a long day trip from Cancun, typically a couple of hours each way. Cenotes, the natural sinkholes with fresh water, are popular on hot days, and many are close to the Riviera Maya. Tulum's ruins sit on a clifftop above the sea and are easy to combine with a beach afternoon.

Weather affects these plans. Morning is often the best time for ruins, before the heat builds. Afternoon showers are more common in the rainy months, so keep cenotes and indoor options in mind for those days, and carry water, a hat and sunscreen. Choose reef-safe sunscreen and avoid touching coral when you snorkel.

## Money, tipping and everyday details

US dollars are widely accepted in tourist areas, but you will usually get a better rate by paying in Mexican pesos, especially at local restaurants and markets. Check your card's foreign transaction fees before you travel, and let your bank know your travel dates. Credit cards are accepted at most resorts and larger restaurants.

Tipping is customary in restaurants, typically around 10 to 15 percent if service is not already included. Many all-inclusive resorts include gratuities or service charges, so read your booking details. Carry small bills for drivers, bag handlers and beach staff.

Water safety advice varies, and many visitors drink bottled or filtered water even at resorts. Ask your hotel what they recommend, and keep a small first-aid kit with any medications you use.

## Cancun for families, friends and couples

Cancun is famous for couples, but its beaches and attractions suit many kinds of travelers. The main difference is the style of resort. Adults-only properties are quiet, spa-focused and designed for two or for groups of adults. Family-friendly resorts add kids' clubs and pools, which can be lively but noisy. If you are traveling as a couple and want calm evenings, filter for adults-only properties and check the pool and restaurant reviews for noise levels.

## Pool, beach and restaurant tips

Beach access is one of the biggest differences between properties. Some resorts have wide sand and gentle entry; others have rocky sections or limited shade. Ask about beach chairs and whether they are included or require an early arrival. Restaurant reservations are worth making at the beginning of your stay, especially for sunset dinners and specialty restaurants, because walk-in tables fill quickly in high season.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Booking the cheapest month without checking sargassum reports.** A low rate can be less appealing on a heavy seaweed week.
- **Assuming every beach is the same.** Facing the open Caribbean or sheltered waters makes a real difference to swimming conditions.
- **Planning too many day trips.** Two outings per week usually leave enough energy for beach time and dinners.
- **Forgetting the sun.** Mexico's sun is strong year-round, and sunburn is one of the most common complaints on the first day.

## Packing list for a Cancun trip

Bring lightweight clothing, a swimsuit cover-up for walking to restaurants, a refillable water bottle, reef-safe sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses, and a light rain layer for summer. Pack a small dry bag if you plan to visit cenotes or go on a boat trip. Bring comfortable walking sandals and one dressier outfit for a special dinner.
