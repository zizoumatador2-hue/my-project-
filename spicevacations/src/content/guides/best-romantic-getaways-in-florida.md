Florida might be famous for theme parks, but it is one of the best places in the country for a romantic getaway. With more than a thousand miles of coastline, islands linked by bridges, historic resorts and warm water most of the year, it can deliver a quick weekend or a once-in-a-lifetime splurge without a passport.

We've organized this guide by the kind of romance you're after: sunsets and island life, pure beach time, classic glamour or quiet nature. If you're still deciding between Florida and other destinations, start with our complete guide to [romantic getaways](/), which compares every major option for couples.

## Best for sunsets and island life

### 1. Key West

Key West sits at the end of the Overseas Highway, closer to Havana than to Miami. It's compact, walkable and full of character: tin-roofed conch houses, tropical gardens and bars that spill onto the sidewalk. The daily sunset celebration at Mallory Square, with street performers and crowds cheering as the sun dips below the horizon, is a Key West ritual. For a quieter version, book a sunset sail.

By day, bike past the Southernmost Point marker, visit the Ernest Hemingway Home and Museum, or take a boat to snorkel on the reef. By night, choose a garden restaurant in Old Town and walk home under the stars.

**Where to stay:** [Casa Marina Key West](/resorts/casa-marina-key-west/) is a historic 1920s resort with one of the island's largest private beaches, a rarity in Key West. Guesthouses in Old Town put you steps from dinner.

### 2. Little Palm Island

If your idea of romance is having nowhere to be and no one around, [Little Palm Island Resort & Spa](/resorts/little-palm-island/) is Florida's most secluded hideaway. It's an adults-only private island in the Lower Keys, reached by boat or seaplane, with thatched-roof bungalows tucked among palms. Dinner is served steps from the water, sometimes with your toes in the sand. It's a splurge, and it's the kind of place couples save for honeymoons and milestone anniversaries.

### 3. Islamorada and Marathon

The Middle and Upper Keys are slower and more nature-focused than Key West. Islamorada is known as a fishing capital, with waterfront restaurants and calm backcountry waters for kayaking and paddleboarding. Marathon is home to the Seven Mile Bridge and turtle rescue programs. This stretch works well as a first stop on a drive to Key West, or as a peaceful weekend on its own. ## Best for pure beach time

### 4. Siesta Key

On the Gulf Coast near Sarasota, Siesta Key is famous for its fine, white quartz sand, which stays cool underfoot and squeaks as you walk. The water is typically calm, perfect for swimming and floating. Evenings bring golden sunsets over the Gulf, and Siesta Key Village has casual restaurants and bars. Sarasota itself adds culture, from the Ringling museum complex to an active arts scene.

### 5. St. Pete Beach

St. Pete Beach is an easy, great-value beach getaway less than an hour from Tampa's airport, depending on traffic. The landmark is [The Don CeSar](/resorts/the-don-cesar/), the pink "Pink Palace" that has overlooked the Gulf since 1928. Nearby, Pass-a-Grille has a relaxed old-Florida feel, and downtown St. Petersburg's waterfront museums and restaurants are a short drive away.

### 6. Sanibel and Captiva

These barrier islands near Fort Myers are all about slowing down. Sanibel is famous for shelling, with beaches where you'll see people bent over in the "Sanibel stoop." Much of the island is protected wildlife habitat, including the J.N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge. Captiva, at the northern tip, is even quieter, with sunsets that draw a crowd every evening. Both islands have been rebuilding after recent hurricanes, so check which accommodations and restaurants are open before you go.

### 7. Scenic Highway 30A and Destin

In Florida's Panhandle, Scenic Highway 30A links a string of planned beach towns with pastel architecture, bike paths and farm-to-table restaurants. The water here has a distinctive emerald tint and the sand is sugar-white. It's especially lovely in spring and fall, when summer crowds thin out.

## Best for classic glamour

### 8. Palm Beach

Palm Beach is Florida's most polished getaway. Stroll the boutiques of Worth Avenue, bike the Lake Trail along the Intracoastal Waterway and spend an afternoon by the ocean. [The Breakers](/resorts/the-breakers-palm-beach/), founded by Henry Flagler and rebuilt in the 1920s in Italian Renaissance style, is one of the state's most famous resorts, with a beach club, spa and many restaurants.

### 9. Miami Beach

Miami offers a completely different kind of romance: Art Deco hotels, rooftop cocktails, Cuban coffee and live salsa. Start the day with a sunrise walk on the beach, spend the afternoon in Wynwood's galleries and end with dinner in the Design District or Coconut Grove. Mid-Beach properties like [Faena Hotel Miami Beach](/resorts/faena-hotel-miami-beach/) feel more grown-up than the heart of South Beach. ## Best for history and charm

### 10. Amelia Island

In the far northeast corner of the state, Amelia Island combines 13 miles of beaches with the Victorian seaport town of Fernandina Beach. Its historic downtown is filled with restored buildings, shops and restaurants, and horseback riding on the beach is a memorable couples activity.

### 11. St. Augustine

Founded in 1565, St. Augustine is the oldest continuously inhabited European-founded city in the United States. Walk its narrow streets, tour the Castillo de San Marcos, and enjoy the city's festive Nights of Lights display during the winter holiday season. Pair it with a beach day on nearby Anastasia Island.

## Best for something different

### 12. Winter Park and Orlando for grown-ups

Orlando can be surprisingly romantic without kids. Spend a day on the rides you actually want, enjoy an EPCOT festival, then retreat to Winter Park's brick streets and take the scenic boat tour through its chain of lakes. The [Four Seasons Resort Orlando](/resorts/four-seasons-orlando/) has an adults-only pool and rooftop dining with fireworks views. ## When to plan a Florida getaway

Florida's weather shapes every trip. **March through May** and **October through November** are usually the best months for couples, with warm days, cooler evenings and lower humidity. **December through April** is peak season in South Florida and the Keys, so book early. **June through September** is hot and humid with frequent afternoon thunderstorms, and the **Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30**. If you travel then, choose flexible rates and consider travel insurance.

## Tips for a romantic Florida trip

- **Don't overdo the driving.** Florida is bigger than it looks; choose one or two bases.
- **Plan around sunsets.** The Gulf Coast and Keys face west, so sunset is a daily event.
- **Book special dinners ahead**, especially on weekends and in peak season.
- **Protect yourselves from the sun.** Use reef-safe sunscreen, especially when snorkeling in the Keys.
- **Consider a midweek stay.** Weekends fill up fast in popular beach towns.
- **Mix two moods.** Pair a lively base like Key West or Miami with a quiet one like Islamorada or Captiva for a trip that feels longer than it is.
- **Pack for the water.** Bring water shoes for rocky Keys shorelines, and book snorkel trips for the morning, when seas are often calmer.

## A sample four-night Florida Keys itinerary

**Day 1:** Fly into Miami and drive to Islamorada. Check in, kayak through the mangroves at golden hour and have dinner on the water.

**Day 2:** Continue down the Overseas Highway, stopping at the Seven Mile Bridge viewpoint. Arrive in Key West by afternoon and join the sunset at Mallory Square.

**Day 3:** Bike around Old Town, tour the Hemingway Home and spend the afternoon on a snorkel or sunset sail.

**Day 4:** Slow morning, beach time at your resort and a long, celebratory dinner in a garden restaurant.

**Day 5:** Fly home from Key West, or drive back to Miami with one last stop for key lime pie.
