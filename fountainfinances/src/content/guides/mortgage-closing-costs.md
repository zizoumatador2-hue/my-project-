Saving for a down payment is only part of buying a home. At the closing table you'll also pay **closing costs**: a stack of lender fees, third-party charges, taxes and prepaid expenses that can add thousands of dollars to what you need in cash. This guide explains what's in them, what's typical and how to pay less. It's part of our [mortgage hub](/mortgage/) and a companion to our [first-time home buyer guide](/guides/first-time-home-buyer-guide/).

## How much are closing costs?

Buyers commonly pay about **2% to 5% of the loan amount**. On a $300,000 home with 10% down, that's roughly:

| Loan amount | 2% | 3.5% | 5% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $270,000 | $5,400 | $9,450 | $13,500 |
| $400,000 | $8,000 | $14,000 | $20,000 |

Where you land depends on your **state and county** (transfer and recording taxes vary widely), your **lender's fees**, whether you buy **discount points**, and how much you prepay for **taxes and insurance**.

## What's included in closing costs

### Lender fees

- **Origination or underwriting fee** for processing and approving the loan, often around 0.5% to 1% of the loan amount
- **Discount points** if you choose to pay upfront for a lower rate (one point costs 1% of the loan amount)
- **Application, credit report and processing fees**

### Third-party services

- **Appraisal** to confirm the home's value
- **Home inspection** (often paid before closing, but still part of your cash needs)
- **Title search and lender's title insurance**, plus optional owner's title insurance
- **Settlement or escrow agent fee** for running the closing
- **Survey, pest inspection or flood certification**, depending on the property and location

### Government fees and taxes

- **Recording fees** to register the deed and mortgage
- **Transfer taxes**, which in some states are paid by the buyer, the seller or split

### Prepaid items and escrow deposits

- **Prepaid mortgage interest** from your closing date to the end of the month
- **Homeowners insurance premium** for the first year
- **Initial escrow deposit** for property taxes and insurance, often a few months' worth

Prepaids aren't really "fees": they're costs of owning the home that you pay upfront. But they still count toward the cash you need to close.

## The two forms that protect you

Federal rules require two standardized forms so you can compare and catch changes:

| Form | When you get it | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan Estimate** (3 pages) | Within 3 business days of applying | Compare offers from several lenders line by line |
| **Closing Disclosure** (5 pages) | At least 3 business days before closing | Compare it with your Loan Estimate and ask about every change |

Some charges can't increase from the Loan Estimate at all, such as the lender's own fees. Others can rise only within limits. If something jumped, ask why before you sign.

## 9 ways to lower your closing costs

1. **Get at least three Loan Estimates.** Lender fees and rates vary more than most buyers expect. Comparing the "Origination Charges" and "Total Closing Costs" lines is the fastest way to save.
2. **Shop for the services you're allowed to choose.** Your Loan Estimate lists services you can shop for, such as title insurance and settlement agents.
3. **Negotiate seller concessions.** In a slower market, ask the seller to pay part of your costs. Loan programs cap how much: for conventional loans it's commonly 3% of the price with less than 10% down, 6% with 10% to 25% down and 9% with more than 25% down; FHA allows up to 6%.
4. **Ask for lender credits.** Accepting a slightly higher rate can fund credits that offset closing costs. It lowers cash due now but costs more over time if you keep the loan.
5. **Close near the end of the month** to reduce prepaid interest (your first payment and escrow math still balance out).
6. **Look for down payment and closing cost assistance.** Many state housing finance agencies and local programs offer grants or forgivable loans for first-time buyers.
7. **Ask about discounts.** Some lenders reduce fees for existing customers, veterans or buyers who complete homebuyer education.
8. **Question junk fees.** Ask what each charge is for and whether it can be waived or reduced.
9. **Ask for a reissue rate on title insurance** if the home was insured recently.

## Closing costs on a refinance

A refinance has many of the same costs: origination, appraisal, title, recording and prepaids. They often total **2% to 6%** of the new loan amount. Unlike a purchase, you can usually **roll them into the new loan**, but that means paying interest on them. Before refinancing, divide total closing costs by your monthly savings to find your **break-even point**, as explained in our [refinancing guide](/refinancing/).

## Plan your cash to close

Your **cash to close** on the Closing Disclosure is the number that matters: down payment plus closing costs, minus any deposits, credits and seller concessions. Lenders also want to see reserves left over after closing. Use our [home affordability calculator](/calculators/home-affordability/) to set a price range, and our [mortgage calculator](/calculators/mortgage/) to estimate your monthly payment including taxes, insurance and PMI.

## The bottom line

Budget about 2% to 5% of the loan amount for closing costs, read every Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure line by line, and compare lenders. A few hours of comparison shopping can save you thousands at the closing table, money that's better spent on your new home or your [emergency fund](/guides/emergency-fund-guide/).
