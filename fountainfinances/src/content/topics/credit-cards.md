## How to compare credit cards

The right card depends on how you will use it. Compare these features before applying:

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Ongoing APR | The interest rate on balances you carry past the due date |
| Intro APR offer | A temporary 0% or low rate on purchases or balance transfers |
| Annual fee | A yearly cost that rewards must outweigh |
| Rewards | Cash back, points or miles, and how easily you can redeem them |
| Fees | Balance transfer, foreign transaction, late and cash advance fees |
| Credit needed | Whether you are likely to qualify |

Card issuers must present key terms in a standardized table in the application, often called the “Schumer box,” which makes side-by-side comparison easier.

## Credit cards for beginners

If you have little or no credit history, a **secured card** — backed by a refundable deposit — or a **starter card** with a low limit and no annual fee can help you build credit. Use it for small, planned purchases and pay the statement in full. See our comparison of [credit cards for beginners](/best/credit-cards-for-beginners/) and read [how to build credit](/guides/how-to-build-credit/).

## Use cards without paying interest

Most cards offer a **grace period**: if you pay the full statement balance by the due date, you are not charged interest on new purchases. Carry a balance, and interest accrues daily at the card’s APR. Learn how in [how credit card interest works](/guides/how-credit-card-interest-works/), or model a payoff with the [credit card payoff calculator](/calculators/credit-card-payoff/).

## Rewards and balance transfers

If you pay in full, [credit card rewards](/credit-card-rewards/) can return a meaningful amount each year. If you carry balances, a [balance transfer card](/balance-transfers/) may save more than any rewards program.
