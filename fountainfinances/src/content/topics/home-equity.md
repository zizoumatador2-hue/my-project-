## What home equity is

**Home equity** is the share of your home you own outright: its market value minus the balance of any mortgages. It grows as you pay down your loan and as your home’s value rises. Equity is a significant part of many households’ net worth.

## Ways to borrow against equity

- **Home equity loan:** a lump sum at a fixed rate with fixed payments.
- **HELOC:** a revolving line of credit, usually with a variable rate, that you draw from as needed.
- **Cash-out refinance:** replaces your mortgage with a larger one and pays you the difference.

Lenders typically limit total borrowing to a set **combined loan-to-value ratio (CLTV)**, often around 80% to 85% of the home’s value.

## Good and risky uses

Borrowing against equity can be reasonable for home improvements that add value, or to replace much higher-interest debt with a plan to repay it. It is risky for discretionary spending, because **your home secures the loan** — if you cannot repay, you could lose it.

## Tax note

Interest on home equity debt may be deductible only if the funds are used to buy, build or substantially improve the home securing the loan, and you itemize, subject to limits. See [IRS Publication 936](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936).
