## How online banks work

**Online banks** operate primarily through websites and mobile apps rather than branches. Without the expense of a large branch network, many pass the savings to customers through higher savings rates, fewer fees and lower minimums. Account opening, deposits (by mobile check capture or transfer), bill pay and customer service all happen digitally or by phone.

## Pros and cons

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Often higher APYs on savings and CDs | No or limited in-person service |
| Fewer monthly and minimum-balance fees | Cash deposits can be difficult |
| Strong mobile apps and fast account opening | External transfers can take 1–3 business days |
| Large fee-free ATM networks or fee reimbursements at some banks | Fewer services such as safe deposit boxes |

## Confirm insurance before you deposit

Look for the bank’s legal name (which may differ from its brand name) and check it using the FDIC’s [BankFind](https://banks.data.fdic.gov/bankfind-suite/bankfind) or the NCUA’s credit union locator. For fintech apps, read the disclosures explaining which partner bank holds deposits and how pass-through insurance applies.

## A common setup

Many people keep a checking account at a local bank or credit union for cash and in-person needs, and hold savings at an online bank that pays a higher rate.
