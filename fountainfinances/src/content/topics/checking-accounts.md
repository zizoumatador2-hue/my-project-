## What to look for in a checking account

A **checking account** is your financial hub: paychecks go in, bills and debit card purchases come out. Because you use it constantly, fees and convenience matter more than the interest rate.

| Feature | What to check |
|---|---|
| Monthly fee | Is there one, and how can it be waived? |
| Overdraft policy | Overdraft fees, nonsufficient-funds fees, and whether you can opt out or link savings |
| ATM access | Size of the fee-free network and any fee reimbursements |
| Deposits | Mobile check deposit, cash deposit options, early direct deposit |
| Digital tools | App quality, alerts, bill pay, Zelle or other transfers |
| Insurance | FDIC (bank) or NCUA (credit union) coverage |

## Traditional, online and credit union checking

- **Traditional banks** offer branches and cash services but often charge monthly fees unless you meet balance or direct deposit requirements.
- **Online banks** frequently have no monthly fees and strong apps, with fewer options for cash.
- **Credit unions** are member-owned and often have lower fees; membership requirements vary.

## Avoiding overdraft costs

Turn on low-balance alerts, link a savings account for overdraft protection transfers and keep a small buffer. Under federal rules, banks must get your consent before charging overdraft fees on most ATM and one-time debit card transactions. Learn more on our [banking fees](/banking-fees/) page.
