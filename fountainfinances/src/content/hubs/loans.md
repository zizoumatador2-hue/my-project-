## Borrowing with your eyes open

A loan lets you pay for something today and spread the cost over time, but that convenience has a price. The two numbers that matter most are the **APR**, which captures the yearly cost of borrowing including many fees, and the **total amount you will repay** over the life of the loan. Two loans with similar monthly payments can differ by thousands of dollars in total cost.

This hub covers [personal loans](/personal-loans/), [auto loans](/auto-loans/), [student loans](/student-loans/) and [debt consolidation](/debt-consolidation/), plus calculators to estimate payments before you apply.

## How installment loans work

Most personal, auto and student loans are **installment loans**: you borrow a fixed amount and repay it in equal monthly payments over a set term. Each payment covers the interest that accrued that month, and the rest reduces the balance. Early payments are interest-heavy; later payments go mostly to principal. Our [loan payment calculator](/calculators/loan/) shows the year-by-year breakdown.

| Loan type | Typically secured by | Common terms | Things to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal loan | Nothing (unsecured) | 2–7 years | Origination fees, APR range by credit |
| Auto loan | The vehicle | 3–7 years | Long terms, dealer add-ons, negative equity |
| Federal student loan | Nothing | 10+ years | Plan choice, eligibility for forgiveness |
| Private student loan | Nothing (often cosigned) | 5–20 years | Variable rates, fewer protections |
| Home equity loan | Your home | 5–30 years | Risk of foreclosure |

## Before you borrow

1. **Know your credit.** Check your [credit reports](/credit-reports/) for errors before applying.
2. **Prequalify where possible.** Many lenders offer rate checks with a soft inquiry.
3. **Compare at least three offers** by APR and total cost, not by monthly payment alone.
4. **Read the fee schedule** for origination fees, late fees and prepayment penalties.
5. **Stress-test the payment.** Make sure it still fits if your income dips.

## Protecting yourself

Legitimate lenders disclose the APR and total cost before you sign, as the Truth in Lending Act requires. Be wary of any lender that guarantees approval regardless of credit, asks for an upfront fee before funding a loan, or pressures you to decide immediately: these are common signs of scams described by the [Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/). If you are struggling with existing debt, a nonprofit credit counselor may be able to help you build a plan.
