To make a matcha latte, sift about 1 teaspoon (2 grams) of matcha into a mug, whisk it with about 2 ounces of 175°F (80°C) water until smooth, then pour in 6 to 8 ounces of warm, frothed milk and sweeten to taste. For an iced version, whisk the matcha with a little water, then pour it over ice and cold milk. It takes about five minutes.

This guide covers hot and iced versions, what to do if you do not own a bamboo whisk, milk and sweetener choices, and how to fix the most common problems.

## What you need

- **Matcha powder.** Culinary-grade matcha is designed for lattes, though ceremonial-grade also works if you prefer a more delicate taste. If you are choosing, read our guide to the [best matcha for beginners](/guides/best-matcha-for-beginners/).
- **Hot water** around 175°F (80°C).
- **Milk** of your choice.
- **A small sifter or fine strainer.**
- **A whisk:** bamboo whisk (chasen), a small electric frother, or a jar with a lid.
- **Optional sweetener:** honey, maple syrup, sugar or simple syrup.

If you are new to matcha and wondering how it differs from regular green tea, see [matcha vs green tea](/guides/matcha-vs-green-tea/).

## Hot matcha latte: step by step

### 1. Sift the matcha

Matcha is a fine powder that clumps easily. Press 1 teaspoon (about 2 g) through a small sieve into a wide mug or bowl. Sifting is the best way to avoid lumps.

### 2. Add water and whisk into a paste

Add about 2 oz (60 ml) of water at roughly 175°F (80°C). Hotter water makes matcha bitter, and the same principle applies to other green teas, as explained in our guide to [water temperature for green tea](/guides/water-temperature-for-green-tea/). Whisk in a quick "W" or zigzag motion until the matcha is smooth, with a light layer of foam on top.

### 3. Warm and froth the milk

Heat 6 to 8 oz (180 to 240 ml) of milk until steaming but not boiling. Froth it with a handheld frother, a steam wand or by shaking it in a closed jar if you have a microwave-safe jar.

### 4. Combine

Pour the milk over the matcha. If you like a layered look, hold back the foam and spoon it on top at the end. Add sweetener to taste.

## Iced matcha latte

1. Sift 1 teaspoon of matcha into a bowl.
2. Add 2 oz of water at 175°F (80°C) and whisk until smooth.
3. Fill a glass with ice and add 6 to 8 oz of cold milk.
4. Pour the matcha over the milk for a layered effect, or stir it in.
5. Sweeten with simple syrup or honey dissolved in a little warm water, because sugar does not dissolve well in cold liquid.

For other chilled tea methods, see our guide to [how to make iced tea](/guides/how-to-make-iced-tea/).

## How to make a matcha latte without a bamboo whisk

You do not need special tools:

| Tool | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| Handheld electric frother | Add the sifted matcha and water to a mug and froth for 15 to 20 seconds |
| Jar with a tight lid | Add matcha and water, shake hard for 15 seconds |
| Small kitchen whisk | Whisk the matcha with a little water in a bowl |
| Blender | Blend matcha with milk for a very smooth, frothy latte |

The bamboo whisk produces the finest foam, but these alternatives produce a pleasant drink.

## Matcha latte ratios

| Strength | Matcha | Water | Milk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mild | ½ teaspoon (1 g) | 2 oz | 8 oz |
| Medium | 1 teaspoon (2 g) | 2 oz | 6–8 oz |
| Strong | 1½–2 teaspoons (3–4 g) | 2–3 oz | 6 oz |

Start with medium and adjust. You can find more on measuring tea in [how much tea per cup](/guides/how-much-tea-per-cup/).

## Choosing milk

- **Whole milk:** creamy and classic.
- **Oat milk:** naturally sweet and creamy, a popular match.
- **Almond milk:** lighter and nutty.
- **Soy milk:** froths well and has a neutral flavor.
- **Coconut milk:** rich, with a distinct flavor.

Barista versions of plant milks froth better. If a plant milk curdles when it meets the hot matcha, warm it a little less or add it more slowly.

## Sweeteners

Matcha has a grassy, slightly bitter and umami flavor. Many people add a little sweetener:

- Honey (dissolve in hot water first for iced drinks).
- Maple syrup.
- Simple syrup.
- Vanilla syrup.
- Sugar-free options if you prefer.

Taste first, then add a little at a time.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitter | Water too hot, too much matcha, or lower-grade powder | Use 175°F water, less powder |
| Clumpy | Not sifted or whisked | Sift and whisk into a paste before adding milk |
| Weak flavor | Too little matcha or too much milk | Add more powder or use less milk |
| Flat color | Old matcha | Replace it; matcha loses color and flavor over time |
| Gritty | Matcha not dissolved | Whisk longer or blend |

Old or poorly stored matcha is a common problem. Read [how to store loose leaf tea](/guides/how-to-store-loose-leaf-tea/) for tips that apply to matcha too: airtight container, away from light and heat.

## Matcha latte variations

- **Vanilla matcha latte:** add a few drops of vanilla extract or a splash of vanilla syrup.
- **Honey lavender matcha:** a little lavender syrup and honey.
- **Strawberry matcha:** layer strawberry purée under the milk.
- **Coconut matcha:** use coconut milk and a pinch of salt.
- **Dirty matcha:** add a shot of espresso.

## Caffeine and nutrition

Matcha is made from whole green tea leaves ground into a powder, so you consume the whole leaf. It contains caffeine, which varies with the amount of powder. The caffeine in a matcha latte is typically lower than in a cup of brewed coffee, but not always. See [caffeine in tea vs coffee](/guides/how-much-caffeine-in-tea-vs-coffee/) for general ranges, and talk to a healthcare professional if you have concerns about caffeine.

## How to buy matcha for lattes

Look for bright green color, a fresh grassy smell, and a fine texture. Dull yellow-brown matcha is usually old or low grade. Buy small tins that you can use within a couple of months. Culinary grade is fine for lattes, while ceremonial grade is made to be whisked with water and drunk plain.

## Make a matcha concentrate for the week

If you make lattes every day, a concentrate saves time. Whisk 4 teaspoons of sifted matcha with about 8 oz of 175°F (80°C) water until smooth, cool it and store it in a sealed jar in the refrigerator for up to 2 or 3 days. Fresh matcha tastes best, and a concentrate can lose its brightness, so make small batches. For each latte, pour about 2 oz of concentrate over ice and milk, stir and sweeten as you like. Shake the jar before using, since matcha settles.

## Cafe-style layering

For a layered iced matcha latte, fill a glass with ice, add milk, then slowly pour the whisked matcha over the back of a spoon so it floats on top. Sweeten the milk, not the matcha, so the sweetener mixes in. For a hot latte with latte art, froth the milk until it has a fine, glossy texture, and pour it into the matcha in a steady stream. The look takes practice, but the taste is the same either way.

## Common substitutions and dietary notes

Matcha is naturally free of milk, so you can adapt the drink to your diet. Use oat, soy, almond or coconut milk for a dairy-free latte. If you avoid sugar, use a small amount of a sweetener that you tolerate, or skip it and choose a smoother grade of matcha. If you are sensitive to caffeine, start with ½ teaspoon and see how you feel; our guide to [caffeine in tea vs coffee](/guides/how-much-caffeine-in-tea-vs-coffee/) explains typical levels. For other green tea drinks, read [water temperature for green tea](/guides/water-temperature-for-green-tea/).
