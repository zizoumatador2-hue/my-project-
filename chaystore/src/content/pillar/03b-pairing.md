## Tea by Time of Day

Tea is flexible, which is part of its charm. Here is a simple way to match types to moments, while remembering that your own preferences matter most.

| Time | Tea ideas | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early morning | Assam, English breakfast, masala chai | Bold, brisk, stands up to milk |
| Mid-morning | Green tea, oolong, jasmine | Lighter, aromatic |
| Afternoon | Darjeeling, Earl Grey, white tea | Gentle, elegant |
| After a meal | Peppermint, ginger, fennel, oolong | Fresh and comforting |
| Evening | Chamomile, rooibos, lemon balm | Caffeine-free and calming |
| Hot day | Cold-brewed green, white, hibiscus | Refreshing and light |
| Cold day | Chai, kahwa, hojicha | Warming and spiced |

If you are sensitive to caffeine, move caffeinated teas earlier in the day and choose from our [caffeine-free tea list](/guides/caffeine-free-tea-list/) in the afternoon and evening. Our guides on [best tea for sleep](/guides/best-tea-for-sleep/) and [tea for digestion](/guides/tea-for-digestion/) give more ideas.

## Pairing Tea With Food

Pairing is about balance. A few simple principles:

- **Match intensity.** Delicate teas suit light foods, and bold teas suit rich foods.
- **Contrast or complement.** Bright, citrusy teas cut through creamy or fatty foods. Toasty teas echo baked flavors.
- **Mind the sweetness.** Sweet desserts can make tea taste more bitter. Choose a tea with some astringency or malt to balance.

| Tea | Pairs well with |
| --- | --- |
| Assam or English breakfast | Toast, eggs, hearty breakfasts, buttery biscuits |
| Darjeeling | Scones, cucumber sandwiches, light pastries |
| Earl Grey | Lemon cake, shortbread, dark chocolate |
| Masala chai | Savory snacks, spiced cakes, fried foods |
| Sencha | Sushi, rice dishes, light seafood |
| Matcha | Mochi, white chocolate, mild sweets |
| Jasmine green | Dim sum, fresh fruit, light salads |
| Roasted oolong | Roasted meats, nuts, caramel |
| Kahwa | Almond biscuits, dried fruit |
| Peppermint | Rich desserts, after a heavy meal |
| Rooibos | Vanilla or nut-based desserts |

### Afternoon tea at home

An easy afternoon tea needs only a pot of tea, a few small sandwiches or scones, and something sweet. Choose a lighter black tea, such as Darjeeling or a blend of Ceylon, or a floral green tea. Warm the pot first, use freshly boiled water for black tea, and follow the steeping times in [how to brew loose leaf tea](/guides/how-to-brew-loose-leaf-tea/).

## Cooking With Tea

Tea is also an ingredient.

- **Smoked and infused dishes:** lapsang souchong can lend a smoky flavor to marinades and sauces.
- **Tea-poached fruit:** simmer pears or figs in strong black tea with spices.
- **Matcha desserts:** matcha works in cakes, cookies, ice cream and, of course, lattes. See our [matcha latte recipe](/guides/how-to-make-matcha-latte/).
- **Tea-infused syrups:** steep strong Earl Grey or hibiscus in simple syrup for cocktails, sodas and desserts.
- **Chai spice:** use masala chai spices in baking, oatmeal and pancakes.
- **Tea-brined meats:** a strong black tea brine adds subtle flavor and color.

When cooking with tea, use fresh, good-quality leaf, because the flavor concentrates.

## Building a Simple Daily Tea Routine

A routine makes tea a pleasure rather than a chore.

1. **Set up a small station.** Keep your kettle, infuser, favorite mug, scale and a few tins together.
2. **Pick a default tea for each part of the day.** You can always change it.
3. **Use a timer.** It takes the guesswork out of steeping.
4. **Take five minutes.** Tea is a good excuse for a short pause.
5. **Rinse your infuser right away.** It keeps gear in good shape.
6. **Rotate your tins.** Use the older tea first.
