## Common Tea Myths, Corrected

Tea attracts a lot of folklore. Here are the most common myths and the more accurate picture.

### Myth: "You must always use boiling water."

Boiling water suits black, herbal and some oolong teas, but it makes many green and white teas bitter. Match the water temperature to the tea. See [water temperature for green tea](/guides/water-temperature-for-green-tea/).

### Myth: "Steeping longer makes tea healthier or stronger."

Longer steeping mainly increases bitterness and astringency, and it increases caffeine extraction to a degree. If you want stronger tea, use more leaf instead. See [how much tea per cup](/guides/how-much-tea-per-cup/).

### Myth: "Green tea has no caffeine."

All true teas contain caffeine, including green and white tea. Decaffeinated tea has much less but not zero. Only herbal teas that do not contain tea leaf are naturally caffeine-free. See [caffeine in tea vs coffee](/guides/how-much-caffeine-in-tea-vs-coffee/).

### Myth: "Herbal tea is always safe because it is natural."

Natural does not mean risk-free. Herbs contain active compounds and can interact with medications or be unsuitable in pregnancy. Check with a healthcare professional when in doubt, and read [is it safe to drink tea every day](/guides/is-it-safe-to-drink-tea-every-day/).

### Myth: "Tea bags are always low quality."

Many tea bags use small leaf pieces, but some premium bags hold whole leaf. Quality depends on the tea, not only the format. Our [loose leaf tea vs tea bags](/guides/loose-leaf-tea-vs-tea-bags/) guide explains.

### Myth: "Chai tea is a type of tea."

"Chai" means "tea." Masala chai is a spiced milk tea, and a chai latte is a café drink. See [masala chai vs chai latte](/guides/masala-chai-vs-chai-latte/).

### Myth: "Expensive tea is always better."

Price reflects labor, rarity and demand, but the best tea is the one you enjoy and brew well. A fresh, modestly priced tea brewed correctly often beats a stale premium tea brewed badly.

### Myth: "You can rinse the caffeine out of tea."

Pouring off a short first steep removes only a portion of the caffeine and does not make tea caffeine-free. If you want none, choose a caffeine-free herbal tea.

### Myth: "Tea cures diseases."

No tea is a cure or a treatment for any disease. Tea can be part of a healthy lifestyle, and general information is available from reputable sources such as the [National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/green-tea). Be skeptical of dramatic claims.

## The Ten Most Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Using water that is too hot for delicate tea.**
2. **Not measuring the leaf.**
3. **Over-steeping.**
4. **Using a tiny infuser that cramps the leaves.** See [best tea infusers](/guides/best-tea-infusers/).
5. **Storing tea in clear jars or near the stove.** See [how to store loose leaf tea](/guides/how-to-store-loose-leaf-tea/).
6. **Buying too much at once.**
7. **Re-boiling water repeatedly.**
8. **Assuming all tea is the same.**
9. **Giving up on re-steeping.**
10. **Not tasting plain first.** Try each new tea without milk or sugar once.

## A Seven-Day Tasting Plan for Beginners

If you want to learn quickly, try this simple plan. Use the same cup and the same amount of leaf each day, and write down notes.

| Day | Tea | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A black tea such as Assam or English breakfast | Maltiness, body, astringency; try it plain, then with milk |
| 2 | A green tea such as sencha | Freshness, grassiness; try two temperatures |
| 3 | An oolong | Floral or toasty notes; try a second steep |
| 4 | A white tea | Subtlety, sweetness |
| 5 | A flavored tea such as Earl Grey or jasmine | Aroma, balance; see [Earl Grey tea](/guides/earl-grey-tea/) |
| 6 | An herbal tea such as peppermint or rooibos | Difference from true tea; no caffeine |
| 7 | Chai or kahwa | Spice and richness; try [chai at home](/guides/how-to-make-chai-at-home/) |

At the end of the week, decide which teas you want more of, which you would skip and what you want to explore next.
